The finiteness part of condition (2) is crucial for what we have just proved,
as the following example shows.
EXAMPLE 4.12.11 Let X be a 2-simplex σ in the plane, with its usual
topology. Break X up into a single open 2-cell Int σ, and infinitely many
open 1-cells and 0-cells, as indicated in Figure 4.12.4. Then X satisfies all the
conditions for a CW complex except the finiteness part of (2). [Condition (3)
is trivial, since the closure of Int σ equals X.] Let Y be the union of the 1-cells
and 0-cells of X, topologized by declaring C to be closed in Y if C ∩eα is
closed in eα for each 1-cell and 0-cell eα. Then Y is a CW complex, but it is
not a subspace of X. For the subspace Bd σ of X is compact, and Y is not.
