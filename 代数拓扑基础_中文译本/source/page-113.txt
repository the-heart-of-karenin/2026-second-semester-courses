Elements of Algebraic Topology
2.3
Barycentric Subdivision
We show here that a finite complex may be “subdivided” into simplices that
are as small as we wish. We will use this result in our consideration of simplicial
homology, and later on in our discussion of singular homology in Chapter 4.
Definition 2.3.1 Let K be a geometric complex in EJ. A complex K′ is said
to be a subdivision of K if
(1) Each simplex of K′ is contained in a simplex of K.
(2) Each simplex of K is the union of finitely many simplices of K′.
These conditions imply that the union of the simplices of K′ equals the
union of the simplices of K. That is, |K′| and |K| are equal as sets. The
finiteness part of condition (2) guarantees that |K′| and |K| are equal as
topological spaces. This is easy to check.
Note that if K′′ is a subdivision of K′ and if K′ is a subdivision of K, then
K′′ is a subdivision of K.
Observe that if K′ is a subdivision of K, and if K0 is a subcomplex of K,
then the collection of all simplices of K′ that lie in |K0| is automatically a
subdivision of K0. We in fact call it the subdivision of K0 induced by K′.
The following result will be useful in the sequel.
Lemma 2.3.2 Let K′ be a subdivision of K. Then for each vertex w of K′
there is a vertex v of K such that
St(w, K′) ⊂St(v, K) .
(∗)
In fact, if σ is the simplex of K such that w ∈Int σ, then this inclusion holds
precisely when v is a vertex of σ.
Proof: If the inclusion (∗) holds, then since w belongs to St(w, K′), so w must
lie in some open simplex of K that has v as a vertex.
Conversely, suppose that w ∈Int σ and v is a vertex of σ. It suffices to
show that
|K| \ St(v, K) ⊂|K| \ St(w, K′) .
The set on the left-hand side of this last inclusion is the union of all simplices of
K that do not have v as a vertex. Hence it is also a union of simplices τ of K′.
No such simplex τ can have w as a vertex, just because w ∈Int σ ⊂St(v, K).
Thus any such simplex lies in |K| \ St(w, K′).
EXAMPLE 2.3.3 Let K consist of the 1-simplex [0, 1] and it vertices. Let
L consist of the 1-simplices [1/(n + 1), 1/n], n = 1, 2, . . . and their vertices,
along with the vertex 0. Then |L| = |K| as sets but not as topological spaces.
In fact L satisfies all the conditions for a subdivision except the finiteness part
of condition (2).
