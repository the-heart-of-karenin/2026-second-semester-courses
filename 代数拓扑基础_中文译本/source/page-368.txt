Theorem 5.9.6 The cochain cup product induces a cohomology operation
Hp(X; R) × Hq(X; R) ⌣
−→Hp+q(X; R)
that is bilinear and associative. The cohomology class {z0} acts as a unity
element.
Proof: If zp and zq are cocycles, then their cup product is a cocycle as well,
since
δ(zp ⌣zq) = δzp ⌣zq + (−1)pzp ⌣δzq = 0 .
The cohomology class of this product depends only on the cohomology classes
of zp and zq, since
(zp + δdp−1) ⌣(zq + δdq−1)
= zp ⌣zq + zp ⌣δdq−1 + δdp−1 ⌣zq + δdp−1 ⌣δdq−1
= zp ⌣zq + (−1)p δ(zp ⌣dq−1) + δ(dp−1 ⌣zq) + δ(dp−1 ⌣δdq−1) .
Finally, {z0} is a unity element, since z0 is already a unity element at the
cochain level.
Theorem 5.9.7 If h : X →Y is a continuous map, then h∗preserves cup
products.
Proof: In fact, the cochain map h# preserves cup products of cochains. We
see this as follows: If T is a singular (p + q)-simplex, then we have
⟨h#(cp ⌣cq), T⟩= ⟨cp ⌣cq, h#(T)⟩
= ⟨cp, h ◦T ◦ℓ(ϵ0, ϵ1, . . . , ϵp)⟩· ⟨cq, h ◦T ◦ℓ(ϵp, ϵp+1, . . . , ϵp+q)⟩
= ⟨h#(cp), T ◦ℓ(ϵ0, ϵ1, . . . , ϵp)⟩· ⟨h#(cq), T ◦ℓ(ϵp, ϵp+1, . . . , ϵp+q)⟩
= ⟨h#(cp) ⌣h#(cq), T⟩.
Definition 5.9.8 Let H∗(X; R) denote the external direct sum Hi(X; R).
The cup product operation makes this group into a ring with a unity element.
It is called the cohomology ring of X with coefficients in R.
In Exercise 4, you will be asked to show that the cup product, as a function
of each variable separately, is an R-module homomorphism. This means that
H∗(X; R) is what is sometimes called a ring with operators R. In the special
case where R is a field F, it is called an algebra over F.
