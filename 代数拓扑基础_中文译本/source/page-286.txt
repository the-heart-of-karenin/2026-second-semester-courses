EXAMPLE 4.12.9 Many of the constructions used in topology for forming
new spaces from old ones, when applied to CW complexes, give rise to CW
complexes. For instance, the product X × Y of two CW complexes is a CW
complex, provided Y is locally compact. Often adjunction spaces can be made
into CW complexes. If A is a subcomplex of X, a cellular map f : A →Y
is a map carrying each p-cell of A into the union of the open cells of Y of
dimension at most p. In this case, one can show that the adjunction space
X ∪f Y is a CW complex.
Definition 4.12.10 Let X be a CW complex. Let Y be a subspace of X that
equals a union of open cells of X. Suppose that for each open cell eα of X
contained in Y , its closure is also contained in Y . Then we shall show that Y
is a closed set in X and that Y is a CW complex in its own right. It is called
a subcomplex of X. In particular, the subspace Xp of X that is the union of
the open cells of X of dimension at most p satisfies these conditions. It is thus
a subcomplex of X, that is called the p-skeleton of X.
We now show that Y is indeed a CW complex. First, Y is clearly Hausdorff.
If eα is an open m-cell of X contained in Y , then its characteristic map
fα : Bm →X carries Bm onto eα, which is contained in Y by hypothesis. The
open cells of X that intersect fα(Bd Bm) = ˙eα must lie in Y ; thus fα carries
Bd Bm into the union of finitely many open cells of Y . It only remains to show
that Y has the topology specified by (3).
Let B ⊂Y . Suppose B ∩eα is closed in eα for each eα contained in Y .
If eβ is a cell of X not contained in Y , then eβ is disjoint from Y . Hence
Y ∩eβ ⊂˙eβ, so Y ∩eβ lies in the union of finitely many open cells of Y , say
e1, e2, . . . , ek. Then
B ∩eβ = [(B ∩e1) ∪(B ∩e2) ∪· · · ∪(B ∩ek)] ∩eβ .
By hypothesis, B ∩ei is closed in ei and hence in X. Therefore, B ∩eβ is
closed in X, and in particular is closed in eβ. Since β was arbitrary, it follows
that B is closed in X, and in particular, is closed in Y , as desired.
