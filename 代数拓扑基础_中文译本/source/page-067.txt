We now show that w ∗K is a complex. The simplices of w ∗K are of
three types: simplices a0a1 . . . ap of K, simplices of the form wa0a1 . . . ap, and
the 0-simplex w. A pair of simplices of the first type have disjoint interiors
because K is a complex. The open simplex Int (wa0a1 . . . ap) is the union of
all open line segments joining w to points of Int (a0a1 . . . ap); no two such
open simplices can intersect because no ray from w contains more than one
point of |K|. For the same reason, simplices of the first and second types have
disjoint interiors.
EXAMPLE 1.9.2 If Kσ is the complex consisting of the simplex σ =
v0v1 . . . vn and its faces, then Kσ = v0 ∗Ks, where s is the face of σ opposite
v0. Thus every simplex of positive dimension is a cone.

One particularly useful consequence of the cone construction is the follow-

Lemma 1.9.4 Let U be a bounded convex open set in Rn, and let w ∈U. If
K is a finite complex such that |K| = U \ U, then w ∗K is a finite complex
such that |w ∗K| = U.
