Elements of Algebraic Topology
EXERCISES
1. Let T be as in Example 8.8.4. Let g : sd T →T be a simplicial
approximation to the identity. Check that if sd γ is the sum of all the
n-simplices of sd T, oriented counterclockwise, then g#(w1) ⌢sd γ
is the cycle c1 pictured in Figure 8.8.2.
2. (a) If ϕ : R →R′ is a homomorphism of commutative rings with
unity, then show that the induced homomorphism
H∗(X; R) →H∗(X; R′)
is a ring homomorphism.
(b) Use the coefficient homomorphism Z →Z/2 to compute the
cohomology rings of P2k, of P2k+1, and of P∞.
3. Show that at any given point in time there are antipodal points on
the surface of the earth at which the temperature and barometric
pressure are equal.
4. Compute the cohomology rings of the following:
(a) S3 × P5 with Z and Z/2 coefficients.
(b) P2 × P3 with Z/2 coefficients.
5. Assume that CP n can be triangulated [and indeed it can be].
(a) Compute the cohomology rings H∗(CP n) and H∗(CP ∞).
(b) Show that if f : CP n →CP n, then the degree of f equals an
for some integer a.
(c) Show that every map f : CP 2n →CP 2n has a fixed point.
(d) Show that there exists no map f : CP 2n →CP 2n of degree −1.
(e) If f : CP 2n+1 →CP 2n+1 has no fixed point, then what is the
degree of f?
6.∗Suppose we form a manifold M by taking two copies of CP 2, delet-
ing a small open 4-ball from each, and pasting the two remaining
spaces together along their boundary 3-spheres. Discuss the coho-
mology ring of M. Compare with the cohomology ring of S2 × S2.
