2.7
Homomorphisms Induced by Homotopic Maps
We now study the important concept of homotopy. Throughout this discus-
sion, I will denote the closed unit interval [0, 1].
Definition 2.7.1 Let X and Y be topological spaces. Two continuous maps
h : X →Y and k : X →Y are said to be homotopic if there is a continuous
map
f : X × I →Y
such that F(x, 0) = h(x) and F(x, 1) = k(x) for all x ∈X. If h and k are
homotopic then we write h ≃k. The map F is called a homotopy of h with k.
We think of F as a means of “deforming” h continuously to k as t varies
from 0 to 1.
We shall prove that if h, k : |K| →|L| are homotopic then the homology
homomorphisms h∗and k∗that they induce are the same. This leads us to
the important result that the homology groups are invariants of the homotopy
type of a space.
