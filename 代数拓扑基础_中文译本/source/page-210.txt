4.3
The Axioms for Singular Theory
We now introduce the relative singular homology groups. Then we show, in
this section and the next, that they satisfy the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms on
the class of all topological pairs.
If X is a topological space and A is a subspace of X, then there is a natural
inclusion Sp(A) →Sp(X). The group of relative singular chains is defined by
Sp(X, A) = Sp(X)/Sp(A) .
The boundary operator Sp(X) →Sp−1(X) restricts to the boundary operator
of Sp(A); hence it induces a boundary operator
∂: Sp(X, A) →Sp−1(X, A)
on relative chains. The family of groups Sp(X, A) and homomorphisms ∂is
called the singular chain complex of the pair (X, A) and is denoted S(X, A).
The homology groups of this chain complex are called the singular homology
groups of the pair (X, A) and are denoted Hp(X, A).
Note that the chain complex S(X, A) is free. The group Sp(X, A) has as
its basis all cosets of the form T + Sp(A), where T is a singular p-simplex
whose image set does not lie in A.
If f : (X, A) →(Y, B) is a continuous map, then the homomorphism
f# : Sp(X) →Sp(Y ) carries chains of A into chains of B, so it induces a
homomorphism [also denoted f#]
f# : Sp(X, A) →Sp(Y, B) .
This map commutes with ∂, so it in turn induces a homomorphism
f∗: Hp(X, A) →Hp(Y, B) .
The following theorem is immediate:
Theorem 4.3.1 If i : (X, A) →(X, A) is the identity map, then i∗:
Hp(X) →Hp(X) is the identity. If h : (X, A) →(Y, B) and k : (Y, B) →
(Z, C) are continuous, then (k ◦h)∗= k∗◦h∗.
