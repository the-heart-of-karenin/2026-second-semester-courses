Definition 1.14.6 A chain map ϕ : C →C′ is called a chain equivalence if
there is a chain map ϕ′ : C′ →C such that ϕ′◦ϕ and ϕ◦ϕ′ are chain homotopic
to the identity maps of C and C′, respectively. We call ϕ′ a chain-homotopy
inverse to ϕ.
We list several properties of chain homotopies; proofs are left to the exer-
cises.
(1) Chain homotopy is an equivalence relation on the set of chain maps
from C to C′.
(2) Composion of chain maps induces a well-defined composition oper-
ation of chain-homotopy classes.
(3) If ϕ and ψ are chain homotopic, then they induce the same homo-
morphism in homology.
(4) If ϕ is a chain equivalence, with chain-homotopy inverse ϕ′, then
ϕ∗and (ϕ′)∗are homology isomorphisms that are inverse to each
other.
(5) If ϕ : C →C′ and ψ : C′ →C′′ are chain equivalences, then ψ ◦ϕ is
a chain equivalence.
Now we investigate what all this means in the special case of augmented
chain complexes.

does ψ. If we extend ϕ and ψ to be the identity in dimension −1, and extend D
to be zero in dimension −1, then D is a chain homotopy between the extended
