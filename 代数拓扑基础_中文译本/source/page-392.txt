As usual, we delete A from the notation if A is empty. We define reduced
homology groups by the equation
Hp(X; G) = Hp({S(X), ϵ}; G) ,
where ϵ is the usual augmentation for S(X).
Since Sp(X, A) is free abelian, the group Sp(X, A) ⊗G is a direct sum
of copies of G. Indeed, if {Tα} is the family consisting of those singular p-
simplices of X whose image sets do not lie in A, then the cosets modulo Sp(A)
of the singular simplices Tα form a basis for Sp(X, A). Therefore each element
of Sp(X, A) ⊗G can be represented uniquely by a finite sum  Tα ⊗gα. This
last sum is our usual way of representing a singular p-chain with coefficients in
G. The minimal carrier of such a chain is the union of the sets Tα(∆p), where
the union is taken over those α for which gα̸ = 0. It is of course a compact
set.
A continuous map h : (X, A) →(Y, B) gives rise to a chain map
h# ⊗iG : S(X, A) ⊗G →S(Y, B) ⊗G .
We sometimes denote this map simply by h#, and the induced homology
homomorphism by h∗. Functoriality is immediate; in fact, it holds on the
chain level.
The short exact sequence of chain complexes
0 −→Sp(A) −→Sp(X) −→Sp(X, A) −→0
splits because Sp(X, A) is free. Therefore, one has a long exact homology se-
quence with coefficients in G. This long exact sequence is natural with respect
to homomorphisms induced by continuous maps.
If the maps h, k : (X, A) →(Y, B) are homotopic, then by the proof of
Theorem 4.3.7 there is a chain homotopy between h# and k#. Then h# ⊗iG
and k# ⊗iG are chain homotopic, so h∗and k∗are equal as maps of homology
with coefficients in G.
Direct consideration of the singular chain complex of a one-point space P
leads to the result that Hi(P; G) = 0 for i̸ = 0 and H0(P; G) ∼= G.
The compact support property of singular homology carries over at once
to the same property for singular homology with arbitrary coefficients.
The only property of singular homology that requires extra care is the
excision property.
Let (X, A) be a topological pair, and let U be a subset of X such that
U ⊂Int A. We know that the inclusion
j : (X \ U, A \ U) →(X, A)
induces an isomorphism in ordinary homology. We wish to show it induces an
isomorphism in homology with arbitrary coefficients as well.
