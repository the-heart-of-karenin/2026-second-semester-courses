Theorem 2.9.3 There is no retraction r : Bn+1 →Sn.
Proof: Property (3) of Lemma 2.9.2 tells us that the identity map i : Sn →Sn
has degree 1. If a retraction r : Bn+1 →Sn existed, then it would be an
extension of the identity map to Bn+1. Property (2) of Lemma 2.9.2 would
then tell us that the degree of i is 0, a contradiction.
Theorem 2.9.4 (Brouwer fixed point theorem) If ϕ : Bn →Bn is a con-
tinuous map, then ϕ has a fixed point, that is, there exists x ∈Bn such that
ϕ(x) = x.
Proof: If ϕ has no fixed point, then we can define a map h : Bn →Sn−1 by
h(x) =
x −ϕ(x)
∥x −ϕ(x)∥
because x −ϕ(x)̸ = 0. Let f : Sn−1 →Sn−1 be the restriction of h to Sn−1.
Then by (2) of Lemma 2.9.2 f has degree 0.
On the other hand, we will now show that f has degree 1. That gives a
contradiction. Define a homotopy H : Sn−1 × I →Sn−1 by
H(u, t) =
u −tϕ(u)
∥u −tϕ(u)∥.
The denominator is non-zero for t = 1 since u̸ = ϕ(u). It is also non-zero
for t < 1 because ∥u∥= 1 while ∥tϕ(u)∥= t∥ϕ(u)∥≤t < 1. The map H
is a homotopy between the identity map of Sn−1 and the map f. Therefore
deg f = 1.
Definition 2.9.5 The antipodal map a : Sn →Sn is the map a(x) = −x.
For our further applications, we need to show that the degree of the an-
tipodal map from Sn to Sn equals (−1)n+1. Below we shall give a direct proof
of the result. In the next section we give an alternative proof. Yet another
proof is given in Section 4.4.
The key result that we need here is the following lemma:
Lemma 2.9.6 The reflection map
ρ(x1, x2, . . . , xn, xn+1) = (x1, x2, . . . , xn, −xn+1)
from Sn to Sn has degree −1.
It may be intuitively clear that the reflection map reverses the orientation of
the tangent plane at each point of the sphere. Nonetheless, we will need to
give a proof in the context of Definition 2.9.1 that deg ρ = −1.
