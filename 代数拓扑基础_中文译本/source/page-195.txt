3.7
∗Categories and Functors
We have seen many references to “induced homomorphisms” and their “func-
torial properties” and to the naturality of the way we assign one mathematical
object to another. This leads us to suspect that there is some common idea
underlying all this language. Indeed there is, and we study it in this last sec-
tion of Chapter 3. It consists mostly of new terminology. The few proofs are
elementary and are mostly left to the reader.
Definition 3.7.1 A category C consists of three things:
(1) A class of objects X.
(2) For each ordered pair (X, Y ) of objects, a set hom(X, Y ) of
morphisms f.
(3) A function, called composition of morphisms,
hom(X, Y ) × hom(Y, Z) →hom(X, Z) ,
which is defined for every triple (X, Y, Z) of objects.
The image of the pair (f, g) under the composition operation is denoted
by g ◦f. The following two properties must be satisfied.
Axiom 1 (Associativity): If f ∈hom(W, X) and g ∈hom(X, Y ) and h ∈
hom(Y, Z), then h ◦(g ◦f) = (h ◦g) ◦f.
Axiom 2 (Existence of identities): If X is an object, then there is an element
1X ∈hom(X, X), the identity morphism, such that
1X ◦f = f
and
g ◦1X = g
for every f ∈hom(W, X) and every g ∈hom(X, Y ), where W and Y are
arbitrary objects.
