We now refine these ad hoc techniques into a systematic method for com-
puting homology groups. This method will apply not only to simplicial homol-
ogy, but to singular homology as well. We introduce in this section a notion of
complex more general than that of the simplicial complex. It was invented by
J. H. C. Whitehead and introduced in [Whi49]. It is called a “CW complex.”
In the next section, we show how to assign to each CW complex a certain chain
complex, called its “cellular chain complex,” which can be used to compute
the homology of the underlying space. This chain complex is much simpler
and easier to deal with than the singular or simplicial chain complexes. In a
final section, we apply these methods to compute, among other things, the
homology of real and complex projective spaces.
Definition 4.12.1 Recall that a space is called a cell of dimension m if it
is homeomorphic with Bm. It is called an open cell of dimension m if it is
homeomorphic with Int Bn. In each case the integer m is uniquely determined
by the space in question.
Definition 4.12.2 A CW complex is a space X and a collection of disjoint
open cells eα whose union is X such that:
(1) X is Hausdorff.
(2) For each open m-cell of the collection, there exists a continuous
map fα : Bm →X that maps Int Bm homeomorphically onto eα and
carries Bd Bm into a finite union of open cells, each of dimension
less than m.
(3) A set A is closed in X if A ∩eα is closed in eα for each α.
The finiteness part of condition (2) was called “closure-finiteness” by J.
H. C. Whitehead. Condition (3) expresses the fact that X has what he called
the “weak topology” relative to the collection {eα}. These terms are the origin
of the letters “C” and “W” in the phrase “CW complex.”
We typically denote eα \ eα by ˙eα. We remark that conditions (1) and (2)
imply that f carries Bm onto eα and Bd Bm onto ˙eα. To see these facts, note
that because fα is continuous, it carries Bm, which is the closure of Int Bm,
into the closure of fα(Int Bm), which is eα. Because fα(Bm) is compact, it is
closed [since X is Hausdorff]. Because it contains eα it must contain eα. Thus
we have fα(Bm) = eα. Finally, because fα(Bd Bm) is disjoint from eα it must
equal ˙eα.
We remark also that the converse of (3) holds trivially: If A is closed in
X, then A ∩eα is closed in eα for each α.
The map fα is called the “characteristic map” for the open cell eα. Note
that the maps fα are not uniquely specified in the definition of a CW complex.
