1.13
Homomorphisms Induced by
Simplicial Maps
If f is a simplicial map of |K| into |L|, then f maps each p-simplex σi of
K onto a simplex τi of L of the same or lower dimension. We shall define
a homomorphism of p-chains that carries a formal sum miσi of oriented
p-simplices of K onto a formal sum miτi of their images. [We delete from
the latter sum those simplices τi whose dimension is less than p.] This map in
turn induces a homomorphism of homology groups, as we shall see.
As a general convention, we shall use the phrase
f : K →L is a simplicial map
to mean that f is a continuous map of |K| into |L| that maps each simplex of
K linearly onto a simplex of L. Thus f maps each vertex of K to a vertex of
L, and it equals the simplicial map induced by this vertex map, as defined in
Section 1.3.
Definition 1.13.1 Let f : K →L be a simplicial map. If v0v1 . . . vp is a
simplex of K, then the points f(v0), f(v1), . . . , f(vp) span a simplex of L.
We define a homomorphism f# : Cp(K) →Cp(L) by defining it on oriented
simplices as follows:
f#([v0, v1, . . . , vp]) =

[f(v0), f(v1), . . . , f(vp)]
if f(v0), f(v1), . . . , f(vp) are distinct,
0
otherwise.
This map is clearly well-defined; exchanging two vertices in the expression
[v0, v1, . . . , vp] changes the sign of the right side of the equation. The family of
homomorphisms {f#}, one in each dimension, is called the chain map induced
by the simplicial map f.
Properly speaking, one should use dimensional subscripts to distinguish
these homomorphisms, denoting the map in dimension p by (f#)p : Cp(K) →
Cp(L). Normally, we shall omit the subscript, relying on the context to make
the situation clear, just as we do with the boundary operator.
