Elements of Algebraic Topology
We apply the preceding lemma to the chain complex E and any two chain
maps f and g from E to itself. The chain complex E vanishes below a certain
dimension [since C and D do]; we may as well take this dimension to be
dimension 1. We know that Ep is free and Hp(E) = 0, for all p. Since any
two chain maps agree trivially in dimension 0, they are chain homotopic. In
particular there is a chain homotopy between the identity map and the zero
chain map. That is, there is a homomorphism D : Ep →Ep+1 satisfying the
equation
∂D + D∂= identity .
Hidden in this formula are all the chain maps and chain homotopies we need.
Recalling that Ep ∼= Dp ⊕Cp−1, we define homomorphisms θ, ψ, λ, and µ
by the equations
D(dp, 0)
=
(θ(dp), ψ(dp))
∈Dp+1 ⊕Cp ,
D(0, cp−1)
=
(λ(cp−1), µ(cp−1)) ∈Dp+1 ⊕Cp .
Then we do some computation. First, we compute
D∂(dp, 0)
=
(θ(∂dp), ψ(∂dp)) ,
∂D(dp, 0)
=
(∂θ(dp) + ϕψ(dp), −∂ψ(dp)) .
Adding these two equations, we obtain the equations
dp
=
θ(∂dp) + ∂θ(dp) + ϕψ(dp) ,
0
=
ψ(∂dp) −∂ψ(dp) .
The second equation says that ψ : Dp →Cp is a chain map, and the first
says that θ : Dp →Dp+1 is a chain homotopy between ϕ ◦ψ and the identity.
Second, we compute
D∂(0, cp−1)
=
D(ϕ(cp−1), 0) −D(0, ∂cp−1)
=
(θϕ(cp−1), ψϕ(cp−1)) −(λ(∂cp−1), µ(∂cp−1)) ,
∂D(0, cp−1)
=
∂(λ(cp−1), µ(cp−1))
=
(∂λ(cp−1) + ϕµ(cp−1), −∂µ(cp−1)) .
Adding the second coordinates of these equations, we obtain the equation
cp−1 = ψϕ(cp−1) −µ(∂cp−1) −∂µ(cp−1) ,
which says that µ is a chain homotopy between ψ ◦ϕ and the identity.
Thus our theorem is proved.
