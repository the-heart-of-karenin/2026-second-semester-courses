Lemma 5.6.10 The Kronecker index is well-defined.
Proof: Let zp and zp be a p-cycle and a p-cocycle, respectively. Let dp+1 and
dp−1 be a (p + 1)-chain and a (p −1)-cochain, respectively. Then we have
⟨zp + δdp−1, zp + ∂dp+1⟩
= ⟨zp, zp⟩+ ⟨δdp−1, zp⟩+ ⟨zp, ∂dp+1⟩+ ⟨δdp−1, ∂dp+1⟩
= ⟨zp, zp⟩+ ⟨dp−1, ∂zp⟩+ ⟨δzp, dp+1⟩+ ⟨dp−1, ∂2dp+1⟩= ⟨zp, zp⟩.
Definition 5.6.11 It is convenient to define the Kronecker map
κ : Hp(C; G) →Hom (Hp(C), G)
to be the map that sends α to the homomorphism ⟨α, · ⟩. Formally, we define
(καp)(βp) = ⟨αp, βp⟩.
The map κ is a homomorphism because the Kronecker index is linear in the
first variable. We leave it to you to check that κ is natural. [See Exercise 2.]
The following lemma is elementary. Its proof makes no use of the theorems
of this section.
Lemma 5.6.12 Let C be a free chain complex. Then there is a natural exact
sequence
0 ←−Hom (Hp(C), G) κ
←−Hp(C; G) ←−ker κ ←−0 .
It splits, but not naturally.
Proof: We shall construct a homomorphism
λ∗: Hom (Hp(C), G) →Hp(C; G)
such that κ ◦λ∗is the identity. It follows that κ is surjective and that the
sequence splits.
Let Bp, Zp, and Cp denote the boundaries, cycles, and chains, respectively,
in C. We begin with the projection homomorphism
π : Zp →Zp/Bp = Hp(C) .
The exact sequence 0 →Zp →Cp →Bp−1 →0 shows that Zp is a direct
summand in Cp. Therefore, π extends to a homomorphism λ : Cp →Hp(C).
Define a chain complex E by letting Ep = Hp(C) and letting all boundary
operators in E vanish. Then
Hp(E) = Ep = Hp(C) ,
Hp(E; G) = Hom (Ep, G) = Hom (Hp(C), G) .
