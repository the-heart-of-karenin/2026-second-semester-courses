Elements of Algebraic Topology
EXAMPLE 4.13.11 Let Sn be an n-sphere. Assume n > 1 for convenience.
We can make Sn into a CW complex having one open cell in dimension n
and one cell in dimension 0. It follows that the cellular chain complex of Sn
is infinite cyclic in dimensions n and 0, and vanishes otherwise. Therefore
Hn(Sn) ∼= Z and H0(Sn) ∼= Z, while Hi(Sn) = 0 for i /∈{0, n}. A similar
computation applies when n = 1.
Exercise 4 will ask you to prove the following theorem. But it will also
offer some guidance.
Theorem 4.13.12 Let C = {Cp, ∂} be a non-negative free chain complex
such that H0(C) is free and non-trivial. Then there is a CW complex X whose
cellular chain complex is isomorphic to C.

3. Let X be a CW complex. Let A be a subcomplex. Show that in-
clusion induces a monomorphism Dp(A) →Dp(X). The quotient
Dp(X)/Dp(A) is denoted Dp(X, A).
(a) Show that if X is a triangulable CW complex, D(X, A) can be
used to compute the simplicial homology of (X, A). [Hint: Use
the long exact sequence to show that inclusion Dp(X, A) →
Cp(X, A) induces a homology isomorphism.]
(b) Show that in general D(X, A) can be used to compute the
singular homology of (X, A). [Hint: Establish that D(X, A)
is isomorphic to the chain complex with pth chain group

from the exact sequence of a triple. Let Xp = Xp ∪A for all in-
tegers p. Repeat the proof of Theorem 4.13.8, replacing Hi(Xp)
by Hi(Xp, A) and Hi(X) by Hi(X, A).]
