The Mayer–Vietoris sequence has many applications. We give one here, as
an illustration.
Recall that if K is a complex and if w0 ∗K and w1 ∗K are cones on K
whose polytopes intersect in |K| alone, then their union is a complex denoted
S(K) and called a suspension of K. [See Section 3.4.] It is easy to show that
the map
π : |K| × [−1, 1] →|S(K)|
defined by

if t ≥0,
(1 + t)x + tw1
if t ≤0,
is a quotient map that collapses |K| × 1 to the point w0, |K| × (−1) to w1,
and is otherwise one-to-one. The proof is similar to the corresponding result
for cones [see Corollary 2.8.8]. This fact motivates the definition of suspension
for an arbitrary topological space.
