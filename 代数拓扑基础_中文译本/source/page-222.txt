Theorem 4.4.8 Let X be a space and let A be a collection of subsets of X
whose interiors cover X. Then the inclusion map SA(X) →S(X) induces an
isomorphism in homology, both ordinary and reduced.
Proof: The obvious thing to do is to define a chain map λ : SA(X) →S(X)
that is a chain-homotopy inverse for the inclusion map. This is not as easy as
one might hope. For any particular singular chain, there is an m such that the
map sdm will work, but as the singular chain changes, one may need to take
m larger and larger. We avoid this difficulty by using a different method.
Consider the short exact sequence of chain complexes
0 −→SA
p (X) −→Sp(X) −→Sp(X)/SA
p (X) −→0 .
It gives rise to a long exact sequence in homology (either ordinary or reduced).
To prove our theorem, it will suffice to show that the homology of the chain
complex {Sp(X)/SA
p (X), ∂} vanishes in every dimension. This we can do. We
need only prove the following:
Suppose that cp is an element of Sp(X) whose boundary belongs to
SA
p−1(X). Then there is an element dp+1 of Sp+1(X) such that cp + ∂dp+1
belongs to SA
p (X).
Note that cp is a finite formal linear combination of singular p-simplices.
In view of Theorem 4.4.5, we can choose m so that each singular simplex
appearing in the expression for sdm cp is A-small. Once m is chosen, let DX
be the chain homotopy of Lemma 4.4.6. We show that cp + ∂DX cp belongs
to SA
p (X). Then we are finished.
