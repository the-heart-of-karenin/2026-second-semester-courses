Exercise 4 will ask you to prove the following theorem. But it will also
offer some guidance.
Theorem 4.14.15 The lens spaces L(n, k) and L(n, ℓ) are homeomorphic if
either
k ≡±ℓ(mod n)
or
kℓ≡±1 (mod n) .
Remark 4.14.16 The converse of Theorem 4.14.15 is true, but the proof is
very difficult.
In a 1935 paper [see [Rei35]], Kurt Reidemeister defined a number associ-
ated with a simplicial complex called its torsion. The torsion is a combinatorial
invariant, which means that two complexes that have isomorphic subdivisions
necessarily have the same torsion. By computing the torsion for lens spaces,
Reidemeister showed that if the simplicial complexes L(n, k) and L(n, ℓ) have
isomorphic subdivisions, then either k ≡±ℓ(mod n) or kℓ≡±1 (mod n).
To complete the proof of the converse, we need to appeal to work of E. E.
Moise from the 1950s. Moise wrote a series of papers concerning “affine struc-
tures in 3-manifolds.” In the fifth of these [see [Moi52]], he proved that two
triangulated 3-manifolds that are homeomorphic necessarily have subdivisions
that are isomorphic.
The homeomorphism classification of lens spaces is thus known. We shall
discuss their homotopy-type classification in Section 8.9.
