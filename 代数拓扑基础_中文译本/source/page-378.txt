It follows that the homology groups and cohomology groups of X are
isomorphic to those of T. On the other hand, their cohomology rings are not
isomorphic. For it is easy to see that the cohomology ring of X is trivial, as
we now show.
Consider the cocycles
w1 = [b, c]∗
and
z1 = [d, e]∗.
Since the cycles w1 and z1 are a basis for the chain group D1(X), the cocycles
w1 and z1 give the dual basis for the cochain group Hom (D1(X), Z). All the
cup products w1 ⌣z1, w1 ⌣w1, and z1 ⌣z1 vanish, because no 2-simplex
has a face in the carriers of either w1 or z1.
The preceding examples should convince you that, as a rule, cup products
are not easy to compute. The difficulty is that one must go down to the
simplicial level and find specific representative cocycles, in order to use the
cup product formula.
