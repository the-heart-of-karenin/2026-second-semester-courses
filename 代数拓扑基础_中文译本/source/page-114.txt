EXAMPLE 2.3.4 Let Σ be the complex consisting of a 2-simplex σ and its
faces. The subdivision K of Bd σ indicated in Figure 2.3.1 can be extended
to a subdivision Σ′ of Σ by forming the cone w ∗K, where w is an interior
point of Σ. The subdivision Σ is said to be obtained by “starring K from w.”
This method of subdividing complexes will be useful in what follows.
Next we describe the starring method for subdividing complexes in general.
We shall need the following lemma, whose proof is left as an exercise.
Lemma 2.3.5 If K is a complex, then the intersection of any collection of
subcomplexes of K is also a subcomplex of K. Conversely, if {Kα} is a col-
lection of complexes in EJ and if the intersection of every pair |Kα| ∩|Kβ| is
the polytope of a complex that is a subcomplex of both Kα and Kβ, then the
union ∪αKα is a complex.
Our method for constructing subdivisions is step-by-step. We now describe
one step in the process.
Definition 2.3.6 Let K be a complex. Suppose that Lp is a subdivision of
the p-skeleton of K. Let σ be a p + 1 simplex of K. The set Bd σ is the
polytope of a subcomplex of the p-skeleton of K, and hence of a subcomplex
of Lp. We denote the latter subcomplex by Lσ. If wσ is an interior point of
σ, then the cone wσ ∗Lσ is a complex whose underlying space is σ. We define
Lp+1 to be the union of Lp and the complexes wσ ∗Lσ as σ ranges over all
(p + 1)-simplices of K. We show that Lp+1 is a complex. It is said to be the
subdivision of Kp+1 obtained by starring Lp from the points wσ.
To verify that Lp+1 is a complex, we note that
|Wp ∗Lσ| ∩|Lp| = Bd σ ,
which is the polytope of the subcomplex Lp of both Wσ ∗Lσ and Lp. Similarly,
if τ is another (p + 1)-simplex of K, then the space |wσ ∗Lσ| and |wτ ∗Lτ|
intersect in the simplex σ ∩τ of K, which is the polytope of a subcomplex of
Lp and hence of both Lσ and Lτ. It follows from the preceding lemma that
Lp+1 is a complex.
