Definition 8.13.11 Let A be a collection of subsets of the space X. We
define an abstract simplicial complex called the nerve of A, denoted by N(A).
Its vertices are the elements of A and its simplices are the finite subcollections
{A1, A2, . . . , An} of A such that
A1 ∩A2 ∩· · · ∩An̸ = ∅.
Now if B is a collection of sets refining A, then we can define a map
g : B →A by choosing g(B) to be an element of A that contains B. If
{B1, B2, . . . , Bn} is a simplex of N(B), then {g(B1), g(B2), . . . , g(Bn)} is a
simplex of N(A), just because ∩Bi is nonempty and contained in ∩g(Bi).
Thus the vertex map g induces a simplicial map
g : N(B) →N(A) .
There is some arbitrariness in the choice of g in the last definition, but any
other choice g′ for g is contiguous to g, since
∩Bi ⊂∩(g(Bi) ∩g′(Bi)) .
