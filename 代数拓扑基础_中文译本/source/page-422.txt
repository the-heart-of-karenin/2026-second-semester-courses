Step 3. To prove (∗), we recall that E−p = Hom (Cp, Z) and apply Step 2
to obtain an isomorphism
E−p ⊗G ∼= Hom (Cp, G) .
The naturality of the isomorphism means that ∂E ⊗iG corresponds to δ. Thus
we have H−p(E; G) ∼= Hp(C; G), as desired.
Step 4. We show that, if A is free of finite rank, then there is a natural
isomorphism
e : A →Hom (Hom (A, Z), Z) ,
defined by the equation
[e(a)](ϕ) = ϕ(a) ,
where a ∈A and ϕ ∈Hom (A, Z).
First, we leave it to you to check that e(a) is a homomorphism of
Hom (A, Z) into Z, and then to check that e itself is a homomorphism. To
prove that e is an isomorphism, choose a basis a1, a2, . . . , ak for A, and let
a1, a2, . . . , ak be the dual basis for Hom (A, Z), as above in Step 2. Let the
corresponding dual basis for Hom (Hom (A, Z), Z) be a1, a2, . . . , ak. All the
groups involved are free abelian of rank k. The homomorphism e carries ai to
ai, since for all j, we have
[e(ai)](aj) = aj(ai) = δij = ai(aj) .
Step 5. To prove (∗∗), we apply Step 4, then Step 2, to define natural
isomorphisms
A ⊗G −→Hom (Hom (A, Z), Z) ⊗G −→Hom (Hom (A, Z), G) .
Setting A = Cp, we have a natural isomorphism
Cp ⊗G −→Hom (Hom (Cp, Z), G) = Hom (E−p, G) .
By naturality, the boundary operator ∂⊗iG corresponds to the coboundary
operator ∂E, so one has a natural isomorphism
Hp(C; G) ∼= H−p(E; G) ,
as desired.
