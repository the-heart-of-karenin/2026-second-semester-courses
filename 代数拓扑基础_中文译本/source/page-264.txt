Corollary 4.10.7 The fundamental group is a topological invariant of a
space.
Since the fundamental group is a topological invariant, if two spaces are
homeomorphic, then their fundamental groups are isomorphic. Contraposi-
tively, this says that if two spaces have different fundamental groups, then
they cannot be homeomorphic.
You might recall the elaborate build-up required before we defined the
simplicial homology of a space, and how we labored to show that the sim-
plicial homology is a topological invariant. Comparatively speaking, studying
homotopy has been quite easy: We defined the fundamental group and proved
its topological invariance in just a few pages. Homotopy theory becomes more
challenging when we seek to compute a fundamental group.
