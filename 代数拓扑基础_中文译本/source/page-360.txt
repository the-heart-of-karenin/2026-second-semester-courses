In the case of homology, finding simplicial cycles of X that generate the
homology is not difficult. Let us represent T and S as quotient spaces of the
rectangle L, as pictured in Figure 5.8.1. The chain d of L that is the sum of
all the 2-simplices of L, oriented counterclockwise, is a fundamental cycle for
(L, Bd L), so its image γ = g#(d) generates the cellular chain group D2(X).
Similarly, the chains
w1 = [a, b] + [b, c] + [c, a] ,
z1 = [a, d] + [d, e] + [e, a] ,
are a basis for the cellular chain group D1(X). As we know, these chains are
cycles representing certain elements of the homology of D(X). Now because
the isomorphism Hi(D(X)) ∼= Hi(X) is induced by the inclusion i : D(X) →
C(X), these same chains, considered now as chains in C(X), represent elements
of the simplicial homology of X.
Cohomology is not so easy. What happens in this case? Of course, we
can find generators for the cohomology of the cellular chain complex D(X).
