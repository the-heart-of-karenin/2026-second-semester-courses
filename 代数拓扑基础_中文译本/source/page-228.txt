Definition 4.5.1 Let G and G′ be functors from C to A, and let X be an
object of C. Let Gp(X) denote the p-dimensional group of the augmented
chain complex G(X). Let M be a collection of objects of C, called models or
model objects.
(1) We say that the functor G′ is acyclic relative to the collection
M if G′(X) is acyclic for each X ∈M.
(2) We say that the functor G is free relative to the collection M if
for each p ≥0 there are
(a) An index set Jp.
(b) An indexed family {Mα}α∈Jp of objects of M.
(c) An indexed family {iα}α∈Jp, where iα ∈Gp(Mα) for each α.
The following condition is to hold: Given X, the elements
G(f)(iα) ∈Gp(X)
are to be distinct and form a basis for Gp(X) as f ranges over all
elements of hom(Mα, X) and α ranges over Jp.
Since Gp(X) has a basis if the functor G is free, that implies that Gp(X)
is a free group [as one would hope when the functor is said to be “free”]. Also,
we shall be constructing functions by first doing the construction on the basis
elements. If these basis elements were not distinct from each other, it might
lead to two constructions producing unequal results for what turned out to
be the same basis element.
EXAMPLE 4.5.2 Consider the singular chain complex functor, from the
topological category to A. Let M be the collection {∆p : p = 0, 1, . . . }. This
functor is acyclic relative to M. We show it is free relative to M: For each p, let
the index set Jp have only one element, let the corresponding family consist
of ∆p alone, and let the corresponding element of Sp(∆p) be the identity
singular simplex ip. It is immediate that, as T ranges over all continuous
maps ∆p →X, the elements T#(ip) = T form a basis for Sp(X).
EXAMPLE 4.5.3 Consider the following functor G, defined on the category
of topological pairs:
(X, Y ) →S(X × Y )
and
(f, g) →(f × g)# .
Let M = {(∆p, ∆q) : p, q = 0, 1, . . . }. Then G is acyclic relative to M, since
∆p × ∆q is contractible. We show it is free relative to M: For each index
p, let Jp consist of a single element, let the corresponding family consist of
(∆p, ∆p) alone, and let the corresponding element of Sp(∆p × ∆p) be the
diagonal map dp(x) = (x, x). As f and g range over all maps from ∆p into X
and Y , respectively, (f × g)#(dp) ranges over all maps ∆p →X × Y , that is,
over a basis for Sp(X × Y ).
