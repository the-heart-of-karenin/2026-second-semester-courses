If m and n are integers and F is a homotopy function between ρm and ρn,
then the lift F ′ of F must satisfy F ′(1, 0) = m and F ′(1, 1) = n. Since F ′(1, u)
is an integer-valued continuous function, we conclude that m = n. Thus the
homotopy classes [ρn] are distinct.
We leave it as an exercise to complete the proof by verifying the equation
[ρm] · [ρn] = [ρm+n].
In contrast to the last theorem, sometimes it is extremely easy to compute
a fundamental group.
EXAMPLE 4.10.9 Consider a space X that is homeomorphic to a convex
subset of a euclidean space; we shall show that π1(X) is trivial. Since the
fundamental group is a topological invariant we may assume the X is a convex
subset of euclidean space. Pick a base point x0. Consider any loop γ1, with
base point x0, and let γ0 be the constant loop at x0. The homotopy map of
Example 4.10.2 applies to show that γ0 ≃γ1. We know that the homotopy
class of γ0 is the identity in π1(X), so π1(X) = 1. [Perhaps we should write
{1} rather than 1 for the trivial multiplicative group, but our notation should
cause no confusion.]
When a topological space X satisfies π1(X) = 1, then we say that X is
simply connected.
