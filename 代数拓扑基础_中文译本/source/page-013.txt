For a two-semester course, the entire book may be appropriate. Of course,
the instructor will have to exercise some discretion in designing a suitable
course. Sections that are optional for the one-semester course (for example,
Categories and Functors) may be necessary for the two-semester course. The
last two chapters are independent of each other. So if it is necessary to cut
some material as the second semester winds down, it can be done.
Regarding the last two chapters, the seventh chapter deals with homologi-
cal algebra, including the universal coefficient theorems and the K¨unneth theo-
rems in various settings. The eighth chapter deals with manifolds—specifically,
the duality theorems associated with the names of Poincar´e, Lefschetz, Alexan-
der, and Pontryagin. ˇCech cohomology is introduced to study Pontryagin du-
ality.
If an instructor wishes to spend less time on the treatment of simplicial
homology, then it is possible to omit Chapter 2. In that case, the topological
invariance of the simplicial homology groups will not be shown directly, but
instead must be obtained via the invariance of the singular homology groups
and the isomorphism between simplicial and singular homology.
Prerequisites
We take it that the student has some familiarity with general topology. This
includes compactness, connectedness, and quotient spaces. The Tietze exten-
sion theorem for normal spaces will be used, and quotient spaces are very
important. Quotient spaces are reviewed at appropriate times.
We also would like the student to know some algebra. Notably some fa-
miliarity with groups, factor groups, and homomorphisms as well as rings,
fields, and vector spaces. No particularly deep theorems will be needed. As
with quotient spaces, basic algebra results are reviewed at appropriate times.
