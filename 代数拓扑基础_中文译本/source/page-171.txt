The zeros at the ends come from the fact that |K| has the homotopy type of
a circle, hence H2(K) = H0(K) = 0. Furthermore, H1(K) is infinite cyclic. It
is generated by the cycle z1 shown in Figure 3.2.2 running around the outer
edge of K or else by the cycle z2 running around the inner edge. These two
cycles are homologous. Because |K0| is topologically the disjoint union of two
circles, H1(K0) ∼= Z ⊕Z and has as basis the cosets of z1 and z2. At the same
time, H0(K0) ∼= Z and is generated by {v1 −v2}.
If i : K0 →K is inclusion, then i∗maps both {z1} and {z2} to the same
generator of H1(K). It follows that (1) i∗is surjective and (2) its kernel is
infinite cyclic and is generated by {z1} −{z2}.
