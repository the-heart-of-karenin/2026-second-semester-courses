2.4
The Simplicial Approximation Theorem
We now show that if h : |K| →|L| is a continuous map, then there is a
subdivision K′ of K such that h has a simplicial approximation f : K′ →L.
The proof when K is finite follows easily from the results of the preceding
section; barycentric subdivision will suffice. The general case requires a slightly
more sophisticated technique of subdivision, called “generalized barycentric
subdivision,” that we will describe below in Definition 2.4.5.
We will have need of the following lemma:
Lemma 2.4.1 (Lebesgue’s covering lemma) For every open cover, A, of
a compact metric space, there exists a positive real number λ such that every
subset of diameter less than λ is contained in some element of A.
The positive number λ is called a Lebesgue number for A.
Proof: If there were no such λ, then one could choose a sequence Cn of sets,
where Cn has diameter less than 1/n but does not lie in any element of A.
Choose xn ∈Cn; by compactness, some subsequence xni converges to some
element x. Now x ∈A for some A ∈A. Because A is open, it contains Cni for
i sufficiently large, contrary to our construction.
Theorem 2.4.2 (The finite simplicial approximation theorem) Let K
and L be complexes, and assume that K is finite. Given a continuous map
h : |K| →|L|, there is an N such that h has a simplicial approximation
f : sdNK →L.
Proof: Cover |K| by the open sets h−1(St w) as w ranges over the vertices of
L. Now given this open covering A of the compact metric space |K|, we let λ
be a Lebesgue number for A.
Choose N so that each simplex in sdNK has diameter less than λ/2. Then
each star of a vertex in sdNk has diameter less than λ, so it lies in one of
the sets h−1(St w). Then h : |K| →|L| satisfies the star condition relative to
sdNK and L, and the desired simplicial approximation exists.
