Application to Relative Homology
Let K0 be a subcomplex of K, and let L0 be a subcomplex of L. Let
f : K →L be a simplicial map that carries each simplex of K0 into a simplex
of L0. We often express this by the phrase
“f : (K, K0) →(L, L0) is a simplicial map.”
In this case, it is immediate that f# maps Cp(K0) into Cp(L0), so that one
has an induced map [also denoted by f#]
f# : Cp(K, K0) →Cp(L, L0) .
This map commutes with ∂and thus induces a homomorphism
f∗: Hp(K, K0) →Hp(L, L0) .
The functorial properties stated in Theorem 1.13.3 carry over immediately
to relative homology.
Definition 1.13.10 Let f, g : (K, K0) →(L, L0) be two simplicial maps. We
say f and g are contiguous as maps of pairs if for each simplex σ = v0v1 . . . vp
of K, the points
f(v0), f(v1), . . . , f(vp), g(v0), g(v1), . . . , g(vp)
span a simplex of L, and if σ ∈K0, they span a simplex of L0.
