1.14
Chain Complexes and Acyclic Carriers
Many of the definitions and constructions we have made within the context of
simplicial complexes also occur in more general situations. We digress at this
point to discuss this more general context. We shall use these results many
times later on. Proofs are left as exercises.
First we define the algebraic analogues to our chain groups. [We mentioned
them earlier in Section 1.12.]
Definition 1.14.1 A chain complex C is a family {Cp, ∂p} of abelian groups
Cp and homomorphisms
∂p : Cp →Cp−1 ,
indexed by the integers, such that ∂p ◦∂p+1 = 0 for all p.
If Cp = 0 for p < 0, then C is said to be a non-negative chain complex. If
Cp is a free abelian group for each p, then C is called a free chain complex.
The group
Hp(C) = ker ∂p/im ∂p+1
is called the pth homology group of the chain complex C.
If C is a non-negative chain complex, an augmentation for C is an epimor-
phism ϵ : C0 →Z such that ϵ ◦∂1 = 0. The augmented chain complex {C, ϵ} is
the chain complex obtained from C by adjoining the group Z in dimension −1
and using ϵ as the boundary operator in dimension 0. The homology groups
of the augmented chain complex are called the reduced homology groups of the
original chain complex C, relative to the augmentation ϵ. They are denoted
either by Hi({C, ϵ}) or by Hi(C). It follows readily that Hp(C) = Hp(C) for
p̸ = 0, and
H0(C) ∼= H0(C) ⊕Z .
[See the exercises.]
An arbitrary chain complex D is said to be acyclic if Hi(D) = 0 for all i.
In particular, the augmented chain complex {C, ϵ} is acyclic if Hi({C, ϵ}) =
Hi(C) = 0 for all i or equivalently if H0(C) ∼= Z and Hi(C) = 0 for i̸ = 0.
