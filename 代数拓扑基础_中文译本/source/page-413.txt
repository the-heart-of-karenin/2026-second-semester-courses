The proof that ϵ is independent of the choice of α and β proceeds as in
the proof of Lemma 7.2.2. Let {α′, β′, γ} be another choice. We treat {α, β, γ}
and {α′, β′, γ} as chain maps of one chain complex A to another A′. There is
a chain homotopy between the corresponding chain maps of A⊗B to A′ ⊗B′.
Thus they induce the same homomorphism
ϵ : H2(A; B) −→H2(A′; B′) ,
and we recognize H2(A; B) = ker(ϕ ⊗iB) while H2(A′; B′) = ker(ϕ′ ⊗iB′).
The homomorphism ϵ is said to be induced by γ and δ, relative to the free
resolutions involved.
Just as in Section 7.2, we see that ϵ depends functorially on γ and δ. That
is, the composite of the homomorphisms induced by (γ, δ) and by (γ′, δ′) is
the homomorphism induced by (γ◦γ′, δ◦δ′). Also the homomorphism induced
by (iA, iB) is an isomorphism.
