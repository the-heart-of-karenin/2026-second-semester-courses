Elements of Algebraic Topology
A space that is the polytope of a simplicial complex will be called a poly-
hedron. [We note that some topologists reserve this term for the polytope of
a finite simplicial complex.]
In general, the topology of |K| is finer [i.e., larger] than the topology |K|
inherits as a subspace of RN. To see this, note that if A is closed in |K| in
the subspace topology, then A = B ∩|K| for some closed set B in RN. Then
B ∩σ is closed in σ for each simplex σ of K, so A ∩σ = B ∩|K| ∩σ = B ∩σ
is closed in σ. By definition A is closed in the topology of |K|.
The two topologies on |K|, the subspace topology and the topology of Def-
inition 1.3.5, are different in general. [See Examples 1.3.6 and 1.3.7.] Nonethe-
less, if K is finite, the two topologies are the same. For suppose K is finite
and A is closed in |K|. Then A ∩σ is closed in σ and hence closed in RN.
Because A is the union of the finitely many closed sets A ∩σ, the set A also
is closed in RN.
EXAMPLE 1.3.6 Let K be the collection of all 1-simplices in R of the form
[m, m + 1], where m̸ = 0 is an integer, along with all simplices of the form
[1/(n+1), 1/n] for n a positive integer, along with all faces of these simplices.
Then K is a complex whose underlying space equals R as a set but not as a
topological space. For instance, the set of points of the form 1/n, n > 0, is
closed in |K| but not in R.
EXAMPLE 1.3.7 Let K be the collection of 1-simplices σ1, σ2, . . . and their
vertices, where σi is the 1-simplex in R2 having vertices 0 and (1, 1/i). See
Figure 1.3.2. Then K is a simplicial complex. The intersection of |K| with the
open parabolic arc {(x, x2) : x > 0} is closed in |K|, because its intersection
with each simplex σi is a single point. It is not closed in the topology |K|
derives from R2, because in that topology it has the origin as a limit point.
