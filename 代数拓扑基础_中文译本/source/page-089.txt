Lemma 1.13.2 The homomorphism f# commutes with ∂; therefore f# in-
duces a homomorphism f∗: Hp(K) →Hp(L).
Proof: We need to show that
∂f#([v0, v1, . . . , vp]) = f#(∂[v0, v1, . . . , vp]) .
(∗)
Let τ be the simplex of L spanned by f(v0), f(v1), . . . , f(vp). We consider
various cases.
Case 1. dim τ = p. In this case, the vertices f(v0), f(v1), . . . , f(vp) are distinct
and the result follows at once from the definitions of f# and ∂.
Case 2. dim τ ≤p −2. In this case, the left-hand side of (∗) vanishes be-
cause f(v0), f(v1), . . . , f(vp) are not distinct, and the right-hand side vanishes
because for each i because at least two of the points f(v0), f(v1), . . . f(vi−1),
f(vi+1), . . . , f(vp) are the same.
Case 3. dim τ = p −1. In this case, we may assume the vertices are ordered
so that f(v0) = f(v1), while f(v1), f(v2), . . . , f(vp) are distinct. Then the left-
hand side of (∗) vanishes because f(v0), f(v1), . . . , f(vp) are not all distinct.
Many of the terms on the right-hand side of (∗) are of the form
(−1)i [f(v0), f(v1), . . . f(vi−1), f(vi+1), . . . , f(vp)] ,
in which the simplex is of dimension p −2. The only two non-zero terms on
the right-hand side are
[f(v1), f(v2), . . . , f(vp)] −[f(v0), f(v2), . . . , f(vp)] ,
and they cancel each other, as desired.
The homomorphism f# carries cycles to cycles, since the equation ∂cp = 0
implies that ∂f#(cp) = f#(∂cp) = 0. Also, f# carries boundaries to bound-
aries, since the equation cp = ∂dp+1 implies that f#(cp) = f#(∂dp+1) =
∂f#(dp+1). Thus f# induces a homomorphism f∗: Hp(K) →Hp(L) of ho-
mology groups.
