For a subset A of a topological space, we will use the notation A to denote
the closure of A, the notation Int A to denote the interior of A, and the
notation Bd A = A \ Int A to denote the boundary of A.
Lemma 1.2.7 Let U be a bounded, convex open set in Rn; let w ∈U.
(a) Each ray emanating from w intersects Bd U = U \ U in precisely one
point.
(b) There is a homeomorphism of U with Bn carrying Bd U onto Sn−1.
Proof:
(a) Given a ray R emanating from w, its intersection with U is convex,
bounded and open in R. Hence it consists of all points of the form w + tp,
where t ranges over a half-open interval [0, a). Thus the point x = w + ap is
a point of R that is in U \ U.
Suppose R intersects U\U in another point, say y. Then we have y = w+bp
for some b > a. We can write
x = (1 −t)w + ty,
where t = a/b. We rewrite this last equation in the form
w = (x −ty)/(1 −t).
Then we choose a sequence of points yn ∈U converging to y, and we define
wn = (x −tyn)/(1 −t).
