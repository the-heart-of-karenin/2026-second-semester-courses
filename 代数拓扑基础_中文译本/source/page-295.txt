Using this fact, we now prove that the cellular chain complex of X can
be used to compute the homology of X. For later purposes, we are going to
prove this theorem in a somewhat more general form. We shall assume that
we have a space X that is written as the union of a sequence of subspaces
X0 ⊂X1 ⊂X2 ⊂· · · .
Then we form the chain complex in which the p-dimensional chain group is
Hp(Xp, Xp−1) and the boundary operator is the boundary homomorphism in
the exact sequence of the triple (Xp, Xp−1, Xp−2). We shall show that under
suitable hypotheses [which are satisfied in the case of a CW complex] this
chain complex gives the homology of X.
Definition 4.13.7 If X is a space, a filtration of X is a sequence X0 ⊂X1 ⊂
· · · of subspaces of X whose union is X. We say that X is filtered by the
sequence of subspaces, and X together with its filtration is called a filtered
space. If X and Y are filtered spaces, a continuous map f : X →Y such that
f(Xp) ⊂Yp for all p is said to be filtration preserving.
