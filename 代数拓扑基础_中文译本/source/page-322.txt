Theorem 5.2.11 Let G be divisible. If
0 −→A −→B −→C −→0
is exact, then so is
0 ←−Hom (A, G) ←−Hom (B, G) ←−Hom (C, G) ←−0 .
The proof of the theorem is left to Exercise 4.
EXERCISES
1. Show that if T is the torsion subgroup of G, then
Hom (G, Z) ∼= Hom (G/T, Z) .
2. Let G be fixed. Consider the following functor from the category of
abelian groups to itself:
A −→Hom (G, A)
and
f −→Hom (iG, f) .
(a) Show that this functor preserves the exactness of
0 −→A −→B −→C .
(b) Show that this functor preserves split short exact sequences.
3. (a) Let m and n be positive integers, and set d = gcd(m, n). Define
hm : Z/n →Z/n to be multiplication by m. Show that the
kernel of hm is generated by {n/d}.
(b) Show that a quotient of a cyclic group is cyclic.
(c) Prove Lemma 5.2.8.
4. Follow the suggested steps to prove Theorem 5.2.11.
(a) Prove it suffices to show that if A ⊂B and ϕ : A →G is a
homomorphism, then ϕ extends to a homomorphism ψ : B →
G.
(b) Prove this fact when B is generated by the elements of A and
a single additional element b.
(c) Let B be a collection of subgroups of B that is simply ordered by
inclusion. Let {ψH : H ∈B} be a collection of homomorphisms,
where ψH maps H into G for each H, such that any two agree
on the common part of their domains. Show that the union of
the elements of B is a subgroup of B, and these homomorphisms
extend to a homomorphism of this union into G.
