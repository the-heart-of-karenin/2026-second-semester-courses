Elements of Algebraic Topology
The basic fact that one needs is that there is a compact, triangulated 3-
manifold M that is a homology 3-sphere, but whose fundamental group π1(M)
does not vanish. The construction can be found in [ST80]. The fundamental
group of M is the icosahedral group.
Now we form the suspension S(M) = M ∗{w0, w1}. This space is a ho-
mology 4-manifold. Since S(M)\{w0, w1} is homeomorphic to the 4-manifold
M × (−1, 1), the local homology conditions are satisfied at all points except
possibly at the suspension points. Since Lk w0 = Lk w1 = M, and M is a
homology 3-sphere, the local homology conditions are also satisfied at w0 and
w1.
Then one needs a separate argument to show that in a triangulated topo-
logical n-manifold, the link of each vertex, while it may not be topologically
an (n −1)-sphere, must have the homotopy type of an (n −1)-sphere. Since
π1(M)̸ = 0, M cannot have the homotopy type of a 3-sphere. Hence S(M)
cannot be a topological 4-manifold.
The proofs of the following theorem and corollary will be left to the exercises.
Theorem 8.3.8 If (X, A) is a triangulated relative homology n-manifold with
X \ A connected, then (X, A) is a relative pseudo n-manifold.
Corollary 8.3.9 If (X, A) be a triangulated relative homology n-manifold,
and if s is a complex of X not in A, then Lk s is a finite complex.
