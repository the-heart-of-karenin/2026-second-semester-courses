Now we consider the zero-dimensional cohomology groups and compute
them.
Theorem 5.3.7 Let K be a complex. Then H0(K; G) equals the group of all
0-cochains c0 such that ⟨c0, v⟩= ⟨c0, w⟩whenever v and w belong to the same
component of |K|.
In particular, if |K| is connected, then H0(K) ∼= Z and that cohomology
group is generated by the cochain whose value is 1 on each vertex of K.
Proof: Note that H0(K; G) equals the group of 0-cocycles, because there are
no coboundaries in dimension 0. If v and w belong to the same component of
|K|, then there is a 1-chain c1 of K such that ∂c1 = v −w. For any cocycle
c0, we must have
0 = ⟨δc0, c1⟩= ⟨c0, ∂c1⟩= ⟨c0, v⟩−⟨c0, w⟩.
