The Universal Coefficient Theorem with Field Coefficients
There is a second version of the universal coefficient theorem that is often
useful. It concerns the case where the coefficient group is a field F. It relates
cohomology with homology if both are taken with coefficients in F.
We have already remarked that if C is a chain complex and F is a field,
then both
Hom (Cp, F)
and
Cp ⊗F
have, in a natural way, the structure of vector spaces over F. Given α ∈F,
and cp ∈Hom (Cp, F), and cp ∈Cp, one defines
⟨αcp, cp⟩= α · ⟨cp, cp⟩,
α(cp ⊗β) = cp ⊗(αβ) .
[See the exercises of Section 5.9 and Section 6.3.] Both δ and ∂are vector
space homomorphisms [i.e., linear transformations], so that
Hp(C; F)
and
Hp(C; F)
also have the structure of vector spaces over F. We show that they are in fact
dual vector spaces.
We recall that if A and B are vector spaces over F, then Hom F (A, B) is
also a vector space over F. It is the set of linear transformations of A into B.
If f : A →A′ and g : B′ →B are linear transformations, then Hom (f, g) is
also a linear transformation. [See the exercises of Section 5.2.]
We wish to reprove the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology in
this context. One difficulty is that we have several possible contenders for the
title of the space of cochains—namely,
Hom (Cp, F) ,
Hom (Cp ⊗F, F) ,
Hom F (Cp ⊗F, F) .
By definition, the first of these gives the cohomology Hp(C; F) of C with
coefficients in F. We shall show that the third does, as well.
Lemma 7.3.4 Let C be a chain complex. Let
ω : Hom (Cp, F) →Hom F (Cp ⊗F, F)
be defined by the equation
⟨ω(f), cp ⊗α⟩= ⟨f, cp⟩· α ,
(†)
where f ∈Hom (Cp, F), and cp ∈Cp, and α ∈F. Then ω is a vector space
isomorphism that commutes with δ.
