Lemma 4.8.10 Let s be a simplex of the complex K. If x and y are points
of Int s, then the local homology groups of |K| at x and y are isomorphic.
Proof: It suffices to prove the theorem when x = s the barycenter of s.
Let sd K be the first barycentric subdivision of K. Let K′ be a subdivision
of K defined exactly as sd K was, except that y is used instead of s when
“starring” the subdivision of Bd s from an interior point of s. There is a linear
isomorphism of sd K with K′ that carries s to y, and carries each remaining
vertex of sd K to itself. Then the pair (|K|, |K|\s) is homeomorphic with the
pair (|K|, |K| \ y).
Recall that a homeomorphism h : |K| →M is called a triangulation of M.
Answering the general question of whether or not an arbitrary manifold can
be triangulated took about a century of work by various mathematicians. The
initial conjecture dates back to an 1899 paper by Poincar´e.1 But it was the
1924 paper [Kne25] by Kneser that gave the problem the name the “Hauptver-
mutung.” The succinct answer to the triangulation question is that all two-
and three-dimensional topological manifolds can be triangulated, while for di-
mensions four and higher there are topological manifolds that cannot be. A
more detailed, but still short, discussion can be found in [Man14].
Theorem 4.8.11 Let M be an m-manifold with boundary. Suppose K is a
complex and h : |K| →M is a homeomorphism. Then h−1(Bd M) is the
polytope of a subcomplex of K.
Proof: If an open simplex Int s of K intersects the set h−1(Bd M), it lies in
this set by the preceding lemma. Since this set is closed, it must contain s.
