Corollary 2.6.3 (The topological invariance of homology groups)
If h : |K| →|L| is a homeomorphism, then h∗: Hp(K) →Hp(L) is an
isomorphism, and the same result holds for reduced homology.
Proof: Let k : |L| →|K| be the inverse of h. Then h∗◦k∗equals (i|L|)∗and
k∗◦h∗equals (i|K|)∗. Thus h∗◦k∗and k∗◦h∗are isomorphisms, so h∗is an
isomorphism.
Application to Relative Homology
We have proved the topological invariance of the (absolute) homology groups.
We can also do the same for the relative homology groups. Everything we have
done so far in this section goes through for relative homology. One simply
replaces each occurrence of a complex by the appropriate pair consisting of a
complex and a subcomplex and applies Theorem 1.13.11, Lemma 2.2.7, and
Theorem 2.5.5. We restate Theorem 2.6.2 and Corollary 2.6.3 in the current
context:
Theorem 2.6.4
(a) The identity map i of (|K|, |K0|) with itself induces the identity
homomorphism in relative homology.

The next definition isolates the often used notion of a retraction. It is the
focus of the exercises. The idea will be strengthened in the next section in
Definition 2.7.10.
Definition 2.6.5 If A ⊂X, then a retraction r : X →A is a continuous map
such that r(a) = a for all a ∈A. If there is a retraction of X onto A, then we
say that A is a retract of X.
