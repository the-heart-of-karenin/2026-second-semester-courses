8.11
Alexander Duality
The Alexander duality theorem is almost as old as the Poincar´e duality theo-
rem. It predates Lefschetz duality by some years. In its original form it dealt
with the relationship between the betti numbers and torsion coefficients of a
subcomplex A of the n-sphere Sn, and the betti numbers and torsion coeffi-
cients of the complement Sn \ A. Nowadays the Alexander duality theorem is
formulated in terms of cohomology and it is proved using Lefschetz duality.
Theorem 8.11.1 Let A be a proper, nonempty subset of Sn. Suppose that
(Sn, A) is triangulable. Then there is an isomorphism
Hk(A) ∼= Hn−k−1(Sn \ A) .
Proof: Assume that n > 0 to avoid triviality. Triangulate the pair (Sn, A).

exact sequence

←−Hk(A) ←−Hk(Sn) .

Combining this isomorphism with that given by δ∗gives us our desired iso-
