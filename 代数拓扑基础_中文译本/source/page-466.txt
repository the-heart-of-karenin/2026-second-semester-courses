8.3
Homology Manifolds
In this section we shall define homology manifolds and obtain some of their
local properties. The class of homology manifolds includes all topological man-
ifolds, so it is a broad and important collection of spaces. The triangulable
homology manifolds will be the basic objects involved in the various duality
theorems.
For convenience, we shall deal with the case of relative homology manifolds
in the present section, even though we shall not actually prove the duality
theorems in the relative case until Section 8.10.
We begin now by recording a convention. We shall be dealing with tri-
angulable spaces. If X is such a space with a specific triangulation, then we
shall frequently abuse notation and make no notational distinction between
the space and the complex triangulating it. For instance, we may refer to a
point of [the space] X or to a simplex of [the complex] X. The meaning in
each instance will be made clear from context.
Definition 8.3.1 A topological pair (X, A) is called a relative homology n-
manifold if for each point x of X not in A, the local homology group Hi(X, X\
x) vanishes if i̸ = n and is infinite cyclic if i = n. In the case where A is empty,
we refer to X simply as a homology n-manifold.
If M is an n-manifold with boundary, then the pair (M, Bd M) is a rela-
tive homology n-manifold (see Section 4.8). More generally, if (X, A) is any
pair such that X \ A is an n-manifold, then (X, A) is a relative homology
n-manifold.
Now if (X, A) is a relative homology n-manifold that is triangulated, then
the local homology properties of X are reflected in the homology groups of the
links of simplices of X. The connection is presented in the following lemma.
