Step 1: We first prove the following elementary fact: Let K be a complex.
Let g : sd K →K be a simplicial approximation to the identity. Then, given
σ ∈K, there is exactly one simplex t of sd σ that g maps onto σ. The others
are mapped onto proper faces of σ.
Recall that if σ ∈K then, since σ lies interior to σ, the map g must carry
σ to one of the vertices of σ.
We now proceed by induction on the dimension of σ. If σ is a vertex v, then
g(v) = v and our result holds. If σ = v0v1, then g(v0) = v0 and g(v1) = v1,
while g(σ) equals either v0 or v1. In either case, g collapses one of the simplices
v0σ or v1σ to a point and maps the other onto v0v1.
In general, let s be a k-simplex v0v1 . . . vk. By the induction hypothesis,
each k −1 face si = v0v1 . . . vi . . . vk of σ contains exactly one k −1 simplex ti
of sd K that is mapped by g onto si. Thus every k-simplex of sd σ is collapsed
by g except possibly for k-simplices of the form σ ∗ti for i = 0, . . . , k. Now g
carries σ to one of the vertices of σ. Suppose that g(σ) = vi. Since
g(ti) = si = v0v1 . . . vi . . . vk ,
the map g carries σ ∗tj onto σ. And for i̸ = j it collapses each simplex σ ∗ti
onto si.
Step 2: Here is another elementary fact: Let K be a complex. Let sd :
Cp(K) →Cp(sd K) be the barycentric subdivision chain map defined in Sec-

p-simplices of sd K lying in σ and suitably oriented.

is the sum of the p −1 faces of σ, suitably oriented. Then sd(∂σ) is by the

and suitably oriented. It follows that
