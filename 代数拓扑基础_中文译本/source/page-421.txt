Theorem 7.6.1 Let C be a free chain complex, and let G be an abelian group.
If C is finitely generated in each dimension, then there are exact sequences
0 ←−Hom (Hp(C), G) ←−Hp(C; G) ←−Ext (Hp+1(C), G) ←−0 ,
0 −→Hp(C) ⊗G −→Hp(C; G) −→Hp+1(C) ∗G −→0 ,
that are natural with respect to homomorphisms induced by chain maps. They
split, but not naturally.
Proof: Step 1. Since Cp is free and finitely generated, the cochain complex
{Hom (Cp, Z), δ} is free. We define a chain complex E by letting
Ep = Hom (C−p, Z)
and taking as the boundary operator ∂E in E the usual coboundary operator
δ of C. One then has the universal coefficient sequences
0 ←−Hom (H−p(E), G) ←−H−p(E; G) ←−Ext (H−p−1(E), G) ←−0 ,
0 −→H−p(E) ⊗G −→H−p(E; G) −→H−p−1(E) ∗G −→0 ,
where for convenience we have replaced p by −p throughout. It is immediate
from the definition that H−p(E) = Hp(C). We shall show that there are natural
isomorphisms
H−p(E; G) ∼= Hp(C; G) ,
(∗)
H−p(E; G) ∼= Hp(C; G) ,
(∗∗)
from which the theorem follows.

Initially, we define Φ on pairs (ϕ, g) in the cartesian product of Hom (A, Z)
and G by the equation
[Φ(ϕ, g)](a) = ϕ(a) · g .
After observing that Φ(ϕ, g) is indeed an element of Hom (A, G), we then note
that Φ is bilinear. Thus Φ can be considered a homomorphism of Hom (A, Z)⊗
G into Hom (A, G).
