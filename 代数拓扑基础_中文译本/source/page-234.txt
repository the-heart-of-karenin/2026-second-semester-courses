Definition 4.6.3 Let X be a space. We define the suspension of X to be the
quotient space of X × [−1, 1] obtained by identifying the subset X × 1 to a
point v and identifying the subset X × (−1) to a point w. The suspension of
X is denoted S(X). The points v and w are called the suspension points of
S(X).
Just as in the case of simplicial theory, one can compute the homology
of a suspension by using a Mayer–Vietoris sequence. One has the following
theorem:
Theorem 4.6.4 Let X be a space. For all p, there is an isomorphism
Hp(S(X)) −→Hp−1(X) .
Proof: Let π : X × [−1, 1] →S(X) be the quotient map. Let v = π(X × 1)
and w = π(X × (−1)) be the suspension points. Let X1 = S(X) \ w and
X2 = S(X) \ v. Since both X1 and X2 are open in S(X), the pair {X1, X2}
is excisive. We will show that X1 and X2 are acyclic. The Mayer–Vietoris
sequence then implies that there is an isomorphism
Hp(S(X)) −→Hp−1(X1 ∩X2) .
We will also show that there is a homotopy equivalence of X1 ∩X2 with X,
so there is an isomorphism
Hp(X1 ∩X2) −→Hp−1(X) .
The theorem follows.
