Similarly, the Jordan curve theorem is much easier to prove in the case
that the imbedding f : Sn−1 →Rn is a simplicial map of some complex whose

maximal rank. The true difficulties appear only when one assumes no more
than continuity and injectivity.
EXERCISES
1. Let M be an m-manifold with boundary, as defined in Section 4.8.
Use invariance of domain to prove the following.
(a) Show that if a point x of M maps to a point of Rm−1 ×0 under
one coordinate patch about x, then it does so under every such
coordinate patch.
(b) Let U be open in Rm and let V be open in Rn. Show that if U
and V are homeomorphic, then m = n.
(c) Show that the number m is uniquely determined by M.
2. (a) Let Y be the topologist’s sine curve [see Definition 4.2.11]. Show
that if h : Y →Sn is an imbedding, then Sn \ h(Y ) is acyclic.
(b) Let X be the closed topologist’s sine curve [see Definition 4.6.5].
Show that if h : X →Sn is an imbedding, then Hi(Sn \ h(X))
is infinite cyclic if i = n −2, and vanishes otherwise.
(c) With X as in (b), show that if h : X →S2 is an imbedding,
then S2 \ h(X) has precisely two components, of which h(X) is
the common topological boundary.
