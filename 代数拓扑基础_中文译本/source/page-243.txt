Elements of Algebraic Topology
Definition 4.8.1 If X is a space and if x ∈X, then the local homology groups
of X at x are the singular homology groups
Hp(X, X \ x) .
The reason for the term “local” comes from the following lemma.
Lemma 4.8.2 Let A ⊂X. If A contains a neighborhood of the point x, then
Hp(X, X \ x) ∼= Hp(A, A \ x) .
Therefore, if x ∈X and y ∈Y have neighborhoods U and V, respectively, such
that (U, x) and (V, y) are homeomorphic, then the local homology groups of X
at x and of Y at y are isomorphic.
Proof: Let U denote the set X \ A. Because A contains a neighborhood of x,
U ⊂X \ x = Int (X \ x) .
It follows from the excision property that
Hp(X, X \ x) ∼= Hp(X \ U, (X \ x) \ U) = Hp(A, A \ x) .
Let us compute some local homology groups.
EXAMPLE 4.8.3 If x ∈Rm, we show that Hi(Rm, Rm \x) is infinite cyclic
for i = m and vanishes otherwise.
Let B denote a ball of positive radius centered at x. By the preceding
lemma,
Hi(Rm, Rm \ x) ∼= Hi(B, B \ x) ∼= Hi(Bm, Bm \ 0) .
Now Sm−1 is a deformation retract of Bm \ 0. See Figure 4.8.1. The formula
for the deformation is given in the proof of Theorem 2.7.11. Therefore, we
have
Hi(Bm, Bm \ 0) ∼= Hi(Bm, Sm−1) .
Theorem 4.4.11 tells us that the group Hi(Bm, Sm−1) is infinite cyclic for
i = m and vanishes otherwise.
EXAMPLE 4.8.4 Let Hm denote the euclidean half-space
Hm = {(x1, x2, . . . , xm) : xm ≥0} .
We have Bd Hm = Rm−1 × 0. If x ∈Bd Hm, then the group Hi(Hm, Hm \ x)
vanishes for all i. If x ∈Hm \ Bd Hm, then Hi(Hm, Hm \ x) is infinite cyclic
for i = m and vanishes otherwise. We prove these facts as follows.
