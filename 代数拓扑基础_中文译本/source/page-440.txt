We show that G′ is free: Given a non-negative integer m let the indexed
family of objects from M corresponding to this integer consist of the single
pair (∆m, ∆m), and let the corresponding element of Sm(∆m, ∆m) be the
diagonal map d : ∆m →∆m × ∆m. We shall show that, as (f, g) ranges over
all pairs of continuous maps,
(f, g) : (∆m, ∆m) →(X, Y ) ,
the elements
[G′(f, g)](d) = (f × g)#(d) = (f × g) ◦d
are distinct and range over a basis for the group Sm(X × Y ). Observe that
(f × g) ◦d : ∆m →X × Y
is just the map T given in coordinates by T(z) = (f(z), g(z)). For each pair
(f, g) of maps, we have a different singular simplex T, and every singular
simplex on X × Y is of this form. Thus these elements do form a basis for
Sm(∆m, ∆m).
Now we show that G is free. Given a non-negative integer m, let the indexed
family of objects from M corresponding to this integer be the family
{(∆p, ∆q) : p + q = m} .
The index set in this case consists of all pairs (p, q) of non-negative integers
for which p + q = m. Let the element of (S(∆p) ⊗S(∆q))m, corresponding
to the index (p, q) be the element ip ⊗iq, where ik : ∆k →∆k denotes the
identity singular simplex on the space ∆k. We need to show that, as (p, q)
ranges over its index set and (f, g) ranges over all maps
(f, g) : (∆p, ∆q) →(X, Y ) , ,
the elements
[G(f, g)](ip ⊗iq) = (f# ⊗g#)(ip ⊗iq) = f ⊗g
are distinct and form a basis for the group (S(X) ⊗S(Y ))m. We know that
Sp(X) is free with the set of all singular simplices f : ∆p →X as a basis,
and similarly, Sq(Y ) has as basis the set of all singular simplices g : ∆q →Y .
By Theorem 6.2.10, Sp(X) ⊗q (Y ) has the elements f ⊗g as a basis, and the
proof is now complete.
