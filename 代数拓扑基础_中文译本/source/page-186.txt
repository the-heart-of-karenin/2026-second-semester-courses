Recall that two maps h, k : (X, A) →(Y, B) are said to be homotopic
(written h ≃k) if there is a map
F : (X × I, A × I) →(Y, B)

Axiom 5: [Homotopy Axiom]
If h and k are homotopic, then h∗= k∗.

Axiom 7: [Dimension Axiom] If P is a one-point space, then Hp(P) = 0 for
p̸ = 0 and H0(P) = Z.
Axiom 8:
[Compact Support Axiom]
If α ∈Hp(X, A), then there is an
admissible pair (X0, A0) with X0 and A0 compact, such that α is in the image
of the homomorphism Hp(X0, A0) →Hp(X, A) induced by inclusion.
A pair (X0, A0) with both X0 and A0 compact is called a compact pair.
Observe that one can modify Axiom 7 by writing H0(P) = G, where G is a
fixed abelian group. What this produces is called “homology with coefficients
in G.” We shall, for the moment, stick with integer coefficients.
