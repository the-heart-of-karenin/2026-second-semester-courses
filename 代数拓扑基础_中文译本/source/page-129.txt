The preceding discussion is independent of the choice of the particular

the preceding lemma that g# is carried by θ. Therefore g# and λ are chain-
homotopy inverses.
We now show that λ is unique. Suppose that λ′ is another augmentation-
preserving chain map carried by λ. Then, by Theorem 1.14.10, there is a chain
homotopy D, also carried by Λ, between λ and λ′. Note that if σ is a p-simplex,
then Λ(σ) = K′(σ) is a complex of dimension p. Since D(σ) is a (p + 1)-chain
carried by K′(σ), it must be zero. Thus D is identically zero. The equation
∂D + D∂= λ −λ′ now implies that λ = λ′.
Step 2: We show the theorem holds if K′ = sd K: For in this case, given
σ ∈K, the complex K′(σ) is a cone. In fact K′(σ) = σ ∗J, where J is the first
barycenetric subdivision of Bd σ. And we know from Theorem 1.9.6 that cones
are acyclic. [This is the place where barycentric subdivisions are essential for
the proof.]
Step 3: We show the theorem is true if K′ = sdNK: In view of Step 1, it
suffices to prove that for any simplex σ of K, the complex sdNK(σ) is acyclic.
This follows from Step 2, which implies that for any complex L,

Step 4: We show the theorem is true in general: In view of Step 1, it suffices
to show that if σ ∈K and if K′ is any subdivision of K, then K′(σ) is acyclic.
Let L = K(σ) and L′ = K′(σ). Then L is acyclic, and we wish to prove
that L′ is acyclic. Let g : L′ →L be a simplicial approximation to the identity.
Choose N so that the identity map of |L| to itself has a simplicial approxima-
tion f : sdN L →L′. For this we need the (finite) simplicial approximation
theorem. Proceeding similarly, choose M so that the identity has a simplicial
approximation k : sdM(L′) →sdNL.
