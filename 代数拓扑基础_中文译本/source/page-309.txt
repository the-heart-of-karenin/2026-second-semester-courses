Lemma 4.14.11 The spaces Sn and Pn can be triangulated so that the an-
tipodal map a : Sn →Sn and the projection map p : Sn →Pn are simplicial.
Proof:
Step 1. We show first that there is a complex L in Rn+1 such that
each reflection map
ρi(x1, x2, . . . , xi, . . . , xn+1) = (x1, x2, . . . , −xi, . . . , xn+1)
induces a linear isomorphism of L with itself, and we show that there is a
triangulation k : |L| →Sn that commutes with each map ρi.
The result is trivial for n = 0. Assume that K is a complex in Rn and
h : |K| →Sn−1 is a triangulation satisfying our hypotheses. Let w0 =
(0, 0, . . . , 0, 1) and w1 = (0, 0, . . . , 0, −1) in Rn+1. Let L = (w0 ∗K)∪(w1 ∗K).
Then ρi induces a linear isomorphism of L with itself, for i = 1, 2, . . . , n + 1.

Step 2. Let k : |L| →Sn be the triangulation of Step 1. The antipodal map

k commutes with a, it induces a homeomorphism of |M| with Pn that is our
desired triangulation of Pn.

It is of interest that they form one of the few classes of spaces that have been
