Such a theory is nowadays called an extraordinary homology theory, or
a generalized homology theory.
It differs from ordinary homology in that a
one-point space may have non-zero homology in many dimensions.
This situation illustrates a phenomenon that occurs over and over again
in mathematics: A theory formulated for one purpose turns out to have con-
sequences far removed from what its originators envisaged.

Definition 3.5.5 Let X = X1 ∪X2. Let A = X1 ∩X2. We say that (X1, X2)
is an excisive couple for the given homology theory if (X1, A) and (X, X2)
are admissible and if the inclusion (X1, A) →(X, X2) induces a homology
isomorphism.
