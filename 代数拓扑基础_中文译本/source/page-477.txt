If X is not connected, then each component of X is itself a homology n-
manifold. In order that X be orientable, it is necessary and sufficient that each
component of X be orientable. A sum of orientation cycles for each component
of X is an orientation cycle for X. We will show that orientability of X does
not depend on the particular triangulation of X. [See Corollary 8.5.5.]
Theorem 8.5.2 (Poincar´e duality—first version) Let X be a compact,
triangulated homology n-manifold. If X is orientable, then for all p there is
an isomorphism
Hp(X; G) ∼= Hn−p(X; G) ,
where G is an arbitrary coefficient group. If X is non-orientable, then there is
for all p an isomorphism
Hp(X; Z/2) ∼= Hn−p(X; Z/2) .
Proof: We shall use the simplicial chain complex C(X) of X to compute
the cohomology of X. Also we use the dual chain complex D(X) of X to
compute the homology of X. There is a one-to-one correspondence between
the p-simplices of X and the dual n −p blocks of X that maps each simplex
to its dual block. Hence the free abelian groups Cp(X) and Dn−p(X) are
isomorphic. This is by an isomorphism ϕ that carries the basis element σ∗for

To define ϕ, we proceed as follows. First, orient the n-simplices of X so
that their sum γ is a cycle. Orient the other simplices of X arbitrarily.
We begin by defining ϕ in dimension n. The oriented n-simplices σ of X
form a basis for Cn(X). Their duals σ∗form a basis for Cn(X). The dual
block D(σ) of the n-simplex σ is the 0-cell σ. We define ϕ(σ∗) = σ. Note that
σ is a generator of the group H0(σ). Thus we have defined an isomorphism
