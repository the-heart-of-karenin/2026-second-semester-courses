Relative Homology and the
Eilenberg–Steenrod Axioms
3.1
Introduction
Until now we have concentrated primarily on studying the “absolute” simpli-
cial homology groups, though we have defined the relative groups and have
proved their topological invariance. Now we shall study the relative groups
in more detail. These groups have many uses. For one thing, they arise nat-
urally in many of the applications of topology. A second point is that they
are involved in an essential way in expressing those fundamental properties of
homology that are called the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms.
One of the ways that relative homology groups can be useful is for giv-
ing information about the absolute homology groups. There are relationships
between the relative groups Hp(K, K0) and the absolute groups Hp(K) and
Hp(K0). For example, the vanishing of Hp(K, K0) and Hp+1(K, K0) implies
that Hp(K) and Hp(K0) are isomorphic. This fact is not obvious. Formulating
this relationship in a precise and general manner is a subtle problem.
In the early days of algebraic topology, the theorems described above were
often awkward and wordy. There was a need for the right language to express
the ideas. A remarkable algebraic idea due to Eilenberg clarified the matter
notably. Really just a new and convenient notation, it is called an “exact
sequence” (of groups, or rings, or some other algebraic structure). The useful-
ness of this concept, in algebra as well as topology, is difficult to overestimate.
Obscure algebraic arguments often become quite transparent once they are
formulated in terms of exact sequences. Other arguments that were difficult
even for professional algebraists become so straightforward that they can be
safely left to the reader.
