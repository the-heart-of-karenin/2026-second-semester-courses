Proof: First, assume K is a simplicial complex. Given two simplices σ and τ
of K we show that if there exists x ∈Int σ ∩Int τ, then σ = τ. Let s = σ ∩τ.
If s were a proper face of σ, then x would belong to Bd σ, which it does not.
Therefore, s = σ. Similarly, s is not a proper face of τ, so s = τ. We conclude
that σ = s = τ.
To prove the converse, we assume (1) and (2′) hold. Let two simplices σ
and τ of K be given. Suppose that σ ∩τ is non-empty. We will show that
σ ∩τ is a face σ′ of σ. Specifically, σ′ is the face of σ spanned by the vertices
b0, b1, . . . , bm of σ that lie in τ. First, σ′ is contained in σ ∩τ because σ ∩τ
is convex and contains b0, b1, . . . , bm. To prove the reverse inclusion, suppose
that x ∈σ ∩τ. Then x ∈Int s ∩Int t, for some face s of σ and some face t of
τ. It follows from (2′) that s = t. Hence the vertices of s lie in τ so that by
definition they are elements of the set {b0, b1, . . . , bm}. Then s is a face of σ′,
as desired. Similarly, we can show that σ ∩τ is a face τ ′ of τ. Thus we have
shown that (2) holds.
It follows from that last lemma that if σ is a simplex, then the collection
consisting of σ and its proper faces is a simplicial complex: Condition (1) is
immediate, and condition (2′) holds because for each point x ∈σ, there is
exactly one face s of σ such that x ∈Int s.
Definition 1.3.4 If L is a subcollection of K that contains all faces of its
elements, then L is a simplicial complex in its own right, called a subcomplex
of K. One subcomplex of K is the collection of all simplices of K of dimension
at most p; it is called the p-skeleton of K and is denoted K(p). The points of
the collection K(0) are called the vertices of K.
Definition 1.3.5 Let |K| be the subset of RN that is the union of the sim-
plices of K. Giving each simplex its natural topology as a subspace of RN, we
then topologize |K| by declaring a subset A of |K| to be closed in |K| if and
only if A ∩σ is closed in σ, for each σ in K. It is easy to see that this defines
a topology on |K|, for this collection of sets is closed under finite unions and
arbitrary intersections. The space |K| is called the underlying space of K, or
the polytope of K.
