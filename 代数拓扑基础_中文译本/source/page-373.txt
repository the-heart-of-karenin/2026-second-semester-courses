Note that in general, if f : K →L is a simplicial map, the cochain map
f # need not preserve cup products on the chain level. That is because the
simplicial cup product is defined using a particular ordering of the vertices,
and the simplicial map f need not preserve the ordering of vertices in K and L.
This would be a serious problem if one wanted to work entirely within oriented
simplicial theory. It would be difficult to prove naturality of the cohomology
cup product.
We remark that the more general cup products
Hp(K) × Hq(K; G) ⌣
−→Hp+q(K; G)
and
Hp(K, A; R) × Hq(K, B; R) ⌣
−→Hp+q(K, A ∪B; R)
exist in simplicial theory just as they do in singular theory. The relative cup
product is in fact easier to define in simplicial theory than in singular theory,
for if cp vanishes on Cp(A) and cq vanishes on Cq(B), then cp ⌣cq automat-
ically vanishes on Cp+q, because a simplex of A ∪B must lie in either A or
B. [Of course, {A, B} is excisive in this case, so the singular cup product is
defined as well. See the exercises of Section 4.7.]
