Step 2: As usual let Cp denote the chain group Cp(K). Let Zp denote the
p-cycles, Bp the p-boundaries. Let Wp denote the group of weak p-boundaries
which are those p-chains some multiple of which is a boundary. Then
Bp ⊂Wp ⊂Zp ⊂Cp .
Since ϕ is a chain map, it carries each of these groups into itself. We shall
show that the quotient groups Cp/Zp and Zp/Wp are free. It will follow then
from Step 1 that
tr(ϕ, Cp) = tr(ϕ, Cp/Zp) + tr(ϕ, Zp/Wp) + tr(ϕ, Wp) .

Step 3: Consider the group Cp/Zp. The homomorphism ∂0 : Cp →Bp−1
obtained by restricting the range of ∂is surjective and has kernel equal to
Zp. Therefore it induces an isomorphism of Cp/Zp with Bp−1. Hence Cp/Zp is
free. Also, because ϕ commutes with ∂, it commutes with this isomorphism.
Hence
tr(ϕ, Cp/Zp) = tr(ϕ, Bp−1) .
(ii)
Step 4: Consider the group Zp/Wp. Consider the projection mappings
Zp →Zp/Bp = Hp →Hp/Tp .
This composition is surjective and has kernel Wp. Therefore it induces an
isomorphism
Zp/Wp →Hp/Tp .
We conclude that Zp/Wp is free. Because the projections commute with ϕ, so
does this isomorphism. Thus
tr(ϕ, Zp/Wp) = tr(ϕ∗, Hp/Tp) .
(iii)
Step 5: Consider the group Wp. We show that
tr(ϕ, Wp) = tr(ϕ, Bp) .
(iv)
Recall that Bp is a subgroup of Wp. Applying the basic theorem of free
abelian groups, we can choose a basis α1, . . . , αn of Wp such that, for some
integers m1, . . . , mk ≥1, the elements m1α1, . . . , mkαk form a basis for Bp.
