Step 2. We prove the following fact:
Let a1, a2, . . . , ak ∈A and b1, b2, . . . , bk ∈B. Suppose the element  ai ⊗bi of
A ⊗B vanishes. Then there are finitely generated subgroups A0 and B0 of A
and B containing {a1, a2, . . . , ak} and {b1, b2, . . . , bk}, respectively, such that
the sum  ai ⊗bi vanishes when considered as an element of A0 ⊗B0.
Recall that A⊗B equals the quotient of F(A, B) by the relations subgroup
R(A, B). The equation  ai ⊗bi = 0 means that the element (ai, bi) of
F(A, B) lies in R(A, B). That is, it can be written as a finite linear combination
of elements of R(A, B), and thus as a finite linear combination of generators
of R(A, B). Recall also that each generator of R(A, B) has the form
(a + a′, b) −(a, b) −(a′, b)
or
(a, b + b′) −(a, b) −(a, b′) .
(†)
Collecting together all the first components of terms in (†) occurring in the
finite expression for (ai, bi), we obtain a finite set A′ ⊂A. Similarly, we
collect together all the second components of terms in (†) occurring in the
finite expression for (ai, bi) to form a finite set B′ ⊂B.
Let A0 denote the subgroup of A generated by A′∪{a1, a2, . . . , ak}, and let
B0 denote the subgroup of B generated by B′∪{b1, b2, . . . , bk}. Then when we
consider (ai, bi) as an element of F(A0, B0), we see that it lies in R(A0, B0).
Thus  ai ⊗bi vanishes when considered as an element of A0 ⊗B0.
