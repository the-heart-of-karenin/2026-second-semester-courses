Theorem 2.10.5 (Lefschetz fixed point theorem) Let K
be a finite
complex and h : |K| →|K| a continuous map. If Λ(h)̸ = 0 then h has a
fixed point.
Proof: Assume that h has no fixed point. Then we will prove that Λ(h) = 0.
Step 1: We show that we may assume that
h(St(v, K)) ∩St(v, K) = ∅for all vertices v of K.
(∗)
To begin, let ϵ = min |x −h(x)|. By the uniform continuity of h, we may
select δ > 0 so that when |x −y| < δ then |h(x) −h(y)| ≤ϵ/3. Let λ =
min{δ, ϵ/2}. Then, for any set A of diameter less than λ, both A and h(A)
have diameter less than ϵ/2, so they are necessarily disjoint. Replace K by a
subdivision of K in which the closed stars have diameter less than λ. Then
the condition (∗) holds. As noted earlier, this does not change the value of
Λ(h).
Step 2: Assume that K satisfies (∗). Let us choose a subdivision K′ of K
such that h has a simplicial approximation f : K′ →K. We show that if s
and σ are simplices of K′ and K respectively, such that s ⊂σ, then f(s)̸ = σ.
Suppose instead that f(s) = σ. Let w be a vertex of s, and let f(w) = v,
a vertex of σ. The fact that s ⊂σ implies that w ∈St(v, K) so that
h(w) ∈h(St(v, K)) .
(∗∗)
On the other hand, we have by the definition of simplicial approximation,
h(St(w, K′)) ⊂St(f(w), K) .
This implies that
h(w) ∈St(v, K) .
