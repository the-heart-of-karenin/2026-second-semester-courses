For later use, we generalize this result to arbitrary free abelian groups:
Lemma 1.12.2 If A is a free abelian group, any subgroup B of A is free.
Proof: The proof given above for the finite case generalizes to the situation
in which A has an infinite basis, as we now show. Using the well-ordering
theorem [which is equivalent to the axiom of choice], we index the basis for A
by a well-ordered set J having a largest element.
We begin by assuming [without loss of generality] that A equals a direct
sum of copies of Z; that is, A equals the subgroup of ZJ consisting of all tuples
(nα)α∈J such that nα = 0 for all but finitely many α. Then we proceed as
before. Since the process may be unfamiliar, we will give the details:
Let B be a subgroup of A. For β ∈J, let Bβ consist of those elements x of
B such that πα(x) = 0 for α > β. Consider the subgroup πβ(Bβ) of Z; if it is
trivial then define xβ = 0, otherwise choose xβ ∈Bβ so the πβ(xβ) generates
the subgroup.

β is the smallest element of J. We prove it in general by transfinite induction.
