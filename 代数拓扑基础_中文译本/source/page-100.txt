EXAMPLE 1.14.3 The chain complex
C(K, K0) = {Cp(K, K0), ∂p}
defined in Section 1.10 is called the oriented chain complex of the simplicial
pair (K, K0). It is both free and non-negative, but in general, it does not have
an augmentation. [For example, in Example 1.10.5 the entire group C0(K, K0)
vanishes, so there can be no surjective map C0(K, K0) →Z.] If K0 is empty,
then the complex C(K, ∅) = C(K) has a standard augmentation, defined by
ϵ(v) = 1 for each vertex of K, as we have seen.
EXAMPLE 1.14.4 If f : K →L is a simplicial map of simplicial complexes
K and L, then f# is an augmentation-preserving chain map of C(K) into C(L).
In general, there exist augmentation-preserving chain maps ϕ : C(K) →C(L)
that are not induced by simplicial maps.
