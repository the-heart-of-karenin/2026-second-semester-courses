Elements of Algebraic Topology
EXAMPLE 4.5.4 Let G be the functor
X →S(X × I)
and
f →(f × iI)# .
Let M be the collection {∆p : p = 0, 1, . . . }. Then G is acyclic relative to
M. It is also true that G is free relative to M, but the proof is not as simple
as in the previous two examples. Here we let Jp be the set of all continuous
functions α : ∆p →I. Let the family {Mα}α∈J be defined by setting Mα = ∆p
for each α. For each α, let iα ∈Sp(Mα × I) be the singular simplex
iα : ∆p →∆p × I
defined by
iα(x) = (x, α(x)) .
As f ranges over all maps of ∆p into X and α ranges over the set Jp, the
element (f × iI)#(iα) ranges over a basis for Sp(X × I).
Note that if G is free relative to a collection M, then it is automatically
free relative to any larger collection. On the other hand, if G is acyclic relative
to M, then it is automatically acyclic relative to any smaller collection. Thus,
if we wish G to be both free and acyclic relative to M, then we must choose
M to be just the right size, neither too large nor too small.
Theorem 4.5.5 (Acyclic model theorem) Let G and G′ be functors from
the category C to the category A of augmented chain complexes and chain
maps of augmented chain complexes. Let M be a collection of objects of C.
If G is free relative to M and G′ is acyclic relative to M, then the following
hold:
(a) There is a natural transformation T of G to G′.
(b) Given two natural transformations S and T of G to G′, then
there is a natural chain homotopy D between S and T.
The proof is sketched in Exercises 1 and 2. If Exercise 1 causes too much
difficulty, you might switch your attention to Exercise 2. Then you might take
some inspiration from the proof of Lemma 4.3.6 and the remarks after that
proof regarding the case p = 0. Once Exercise 2 is understood, return to
Exercise 1.
The method of acyclic models was first formalized by Eilenberg and
MacLane in [EM53]. In the immediately following paper in the same issue
of the American Journal of Mathematics, it was applied by Eilenberg and
Zilber in [EZ53].
Corollary 4.5.6 Let C be a category, and let G and G′ be functors from C to
A. If G and G′ are free and acyclic relative to the collection M of objects of C,
then there is a natural transformation T : G →G′; any such transformation
is a natural equivalence.
