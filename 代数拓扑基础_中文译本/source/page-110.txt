Theorem 2.2.5 Let h : |K| →|L| and k : |L| →|M| have simplicial approx-
imations f : K →L and g : L →M respectively. Then g ◦f is a simplicial
approximation to k ◦h.
Proof: Certainly g ◦f is a simplicial map. If v is a vertex of K, then
h(St v) ⊂St f(v)
just because f is a simplicial approximation to h. Therefore
k(h(St v)) ⊂k(St f(v)) ⊂St g(f(v))
because g is a simplicial approximation to k.
EXAMPLE 2.2.6 Let K and L be the complexes shown in Figure 2.2.1.
Note that the underlying spaces are homeomorphic to the circle and the
annulus respectively. Let K′ be the complex obtained from K by inserting
vertices as shown. Let h be the indicated continuous map, where we denote
h(a) by A, and similarly for the other vertices.
Now h does not satisfy the star condition relative to K and L, but it
does satisfy the star condition relative to K′ and L. Thus h has a simplicial
approximation f : K′ →L. One such is shown in the figure. We denote f(a)
by A′ and similarly for the other vertices.
If h : |K| →|L| satisfies the star condition relative to K and L, then there
is a well-defined homomorphism
h∗: Hp(K) →Hp(L)
obtained by setting h∗= f∗, where f is any simplicial approximation to h. It
is straightforward to see that the “functorial properties” are satisfied.
In general an arbitrary continuous map h : |K| →|L| will not satisfy
the star condition relative to K and L. Hence we cannot obtain an induced
homomorphism h∗as above. What can we do instead? Two ideas are needed.
First we show that, given h : |K| →|L|, it is possible to “subdivide” K,
forming a new complex K′, with the same underlying space as K, so that h
does satisfy the star condition relative to K′ and L. [This is what we did in
the preceding Example 2.2.6.] This step is geometric in nature, and is carried
out in Sections 2.3 and 2.4.
