Definition 4.8.5 A nonempty Hausdorff space X is called an m-manifold if
each point of X has a neighborhood homeomorphic with an open subset of
euclidean space Rm. It is called an m-manifold with boundary if each point of
X has a neighborhood homeomorphic with an open subset of the euclidean
half-space Hm.
Note that an m-manifold is automatically an m-manifold with boundary.
For if x has a neighborhood homeomorphic with an open set in Rm, it has a
neighborhood U homeomorphic with an open ball in Rm. Then U is homeo-
morphic with the open unit ball in Rm centered at (0, 0, . . . , 0, 1), which is an
open set of Hm.
One often includes in the definition of a manifold the requirement that X
have a countable basis, or at least that X be metrizable. We shall not make
either assumption.
