If G is a free abelian group with basis e1, e2, . . . , en, and if ϕ : G →G is a
homomorphism, then we define the trace of ϕ to be the number tr A, where A
is the matrix of ϕ relative to the given basis. This number is independent of the
choice of basis, since the matrix of ϕ relative to another basis equals B−1AB
for some square matrix B, and tr(B−1(AB)) = tr((AB)B−1) = tr(A). The
same argument shows that if i : G →G is an isomorphism, then tr(i◦ϕ◦i−1) =
tr(ϕ).
If K is a finite complex, and if ϕ : Cp(K) →Cp(K) is a chain map, then
since Cp(K) is free abelian with finite rank, the trace of ϕ is defined. We denote
it by tr(ϕ, Cp(K)). The group Hp(K) is not necessarily free abelian, but if
Tp(K) is its torsion subgroup, then Hp(K)/Tp(K) is free abelian. Furthermore,
ϕ∗induces a homomorphism of this group with itself. We use the notation
tr(ϕ∗, Hp(K)/Tp(K)) to denote the trace of the induced homomorphism.
There is no obvious relation between the two numbers tr(ϕ, Cp(K)) and
tr(ϕ∗, Hp(K)/Tp(K)). As a result, the following formula is rather striking.
