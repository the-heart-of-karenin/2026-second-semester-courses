The next theorem describes the geometric motivation underlying our def-
inition of C ⊗C′.
Theorem 7.7.3 If K and L are simplicial complexes, and if |K| × |L| is
triangulated so that each cell σ × τ is the polytope of a subcomplex, then
C(K) ⊗C(L) ∼= D(K × L) .
Thus C(K) ⊗C(L) can be used to compute the homology of |K| × |L|.
Proof: Let D(K × L) denote the cellular chain complex of the CW complex
X = |K| × |L|, and let M denote the underlying simplicial complex of X.
Because X is triangulated, we can use simplicial homology in the definition
of the chain group Dm(K × L). This chain group is a free abelian group, with
one basis element of each cell σp × τ q for which p + q = m. This basis element
is a fundamental cycle for σp × τ q.
When we orient the simplices of K and L, they form bases for C(K) and
C(L), respectively. A basis for the m-dimensional chain group of C(K) ⊗C(L)
consists of all the elements σp ⊗τ q for which p + q = m.
It follows that the groups
(C(K) ⊗C(L))m
and
Dm(K × L)
are isomorphic, for they are both free abelian and there is a one-to-one corre-
spondence ϕ between their bases. That correspondence ϕ assigns to the basis
element σp ⊗τ q of (C(K) ⊗C(L))m a fundamental cycle for the (p + q)-cell
σ × τ. We need to show that this isomorphism can be chosen so that it pre-
serves boundary operators, and thus is an isomorphism of chain complexes as
well.
We now proceed by induction. A basis element for (C(K)⊗C(L))0 is of the
form v ⊗w, where v is a vertex of K and w is a vertex of L. Define ϕ(v ⊗w)
to be a fundamental 0-cycle for v × w, that is, ϕ(v ⊗w) = v × w.
The augmentation ¯ϵ for C(K) ⊗C(L) satisfies the equation ¯ϵ(v ⊗w) = 1
whenever v is a vertex of K and w is a vertex of L. On the other hand, the
chain complex D(K × L) is augmented as a subchain complex of C(M), so
ϵ(v × w) = 1. Thus the map ϕ is augmentation-preserving, since ϵ ◦ϕ = ¯ϵ
holds for 0-chains.
For the induction step, we assume that
ϕ : (C(K) ⊗C(L))i →Di(K × L)
is defined in dimensions i less than m, where m ≥1, and that ϕ ◦¯∂= ∂◦ϕ in
those dimensions. We suppose further that for p+q < m, the chain ϕ(σp ⊗τ q)
is a fundamental cycle for the cell σp × τ q.
