Remark 7.4.6 We have studied the Ext functor and the torsion product
functor only for abelian groups. They are derived from the functors Hom and
⊗, respectively. If, instead of abelian groups, one deals with modules over a
ring R, then one has the functors HomR and ⊗R [mentioned in Section 6.2].
It is natural to ask whether one can also introduce the Ext functor and the
torsion product functor for modules. The answers to these questions are a
large part of the subject matter of homological algebra. [See [ML63].]
Much as in the case of abelian groups, one begins with the notion of a free
R-module. Then, given an R-module A, one lets F0 denote the free module
generated by the elements of A. There is a natural epimorphism
ϕ : F0 →A .
The difficulty that immediately arises is that in the case of a general ring R,
a submodule of a free module is not necessarily free. Thus there need be no
short exact sequence
0 −→S −→F −→A −→0 ,
where S and F are free. Nevertheless, one can let F1 be the free R-module
generated by the elements of ker ϕ and thereby obtain an exact sequence
F1 −→F0 −→A −→0 ,
where F1 and F0 are free. Continuing in this way, one obtains an exact se-
