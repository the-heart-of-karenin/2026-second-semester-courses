In terms of these basis elements, it is easy to compute the boundary oper-
ator in the cellular chain complex D(X). We first compute ∂in the complex
L as follows:
∂c1 = v4 −v1,
∂c2 = v2 −v1,
∂d = −c1 + c2 + c3 −c4.
Applying g#, we see that ∂w1 = g#(∂c1) = 0 and ∂z1 = g#(∂c2) = 0 for both
the torus and the Klein bottle. In the case of the torus, ∂γ = g#(∂d) = 0
because g#(c1) = g#(c3) and g#(c2) = g#(c4). In the case of the Klein bottle,
∂γ = g#(∂d) = 2g#(c2) = 2z1
because g#(c1) = g#(c3) and g#(c2) = −g#(c4).
Thus the homology of the cellular chain complex D(X) when X is the
torus is
H2(X) ∼= Z,
H1(X) ∼= Z ⊕Z,
H0(X) ∼= Z,
while in the case of the Klein bottle, it is
H2(X) = 0,
H1(X) ∼= Z ⊕Z/2,
H0(X) ∼= Z.
Of course, we have carried out these same computations before. But now
their justification comes from our general theorems about CW complexes,
rather than from the ad hoc arguments we used back in Section 1.7. It is in
this sense that our results about CW complexes make systematic the ad hoc
computational methods we studied there.
