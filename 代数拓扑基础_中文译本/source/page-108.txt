Topological Invariance of the Homology

2.1
Introduction
In Chapter 1, we defined a function which assigns to each simplicial complex
K a sequence of abelian groups called the homology groups of K. Our purpose
now is to show that these groups are determined by the topology of K.
We shall attack this problem by studying continuous maps from one
polyhedron to another; we are particularly interested in what such a map
does to the homology groups. We will in fact prove that a continuous map
h : |K| →|L| of polyhedra induces, in a canonical fashion, a homomorphism
h∗: Hp(K) →Hp(L) of the homology groups of the corresponding simplicial
complexes. Constructing this homomorphism will entail a fair bit of work.
We will see that when h is a homeomorphism of topological spaces, then
h∗is an isomorphism of groups. The topological invariance of the simplicial
homology groups is an immediate consequence.
We can see intuitively why there should be an induced homomorphism of
homology groups. If we think of a homology class as a geometric object, then
it is plausible that its image under h should be a well-defined homology class.
For instance, if X is a topological space, then a closed loop on the surface
T is mapped by h : T →X into a closed loop in X. To make this intuition
rigorous will be our task for some time.
We know that a simplicial map f : |K| →|L| induces a homomorphism of
homology groups. The current chapter is dedicated to the task of showing that
an arbitrary continuous map h can be suitably approximated by a simplicial
map f, and that the resulting induced homomorphism depends only on the
map h, and not on the particular approximation chosen.
