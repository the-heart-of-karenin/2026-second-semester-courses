Proof: Suppose that aij is a minimal entry of A that fails to divide some
entry of A. If the entry aij fails to divide some entry akj in its column, then
we use Euclid’s division lemma to write
akj = q aij + r
where the remainder r satisfies 0 < |r| < |aij|. Signs do not matter here; q
and r may be either positive or negative. We then replace (row k) of A by
(row i) −q (row k). The result is to replace the entry akj in the kth row and
jth column of A by akj −qaij = r. The value of α for this new matrix is at
most |r|, which is less than α(A).
A similar argument applies if aij fails to divide some entry in its row.

To prove the other direction, suppose that α(A) divides every entry of A.
Any matrix B obtained by applying elementary operations to A will be non-
zero and α(A) will also divide each entry of B. Choose any non-zero entry bij
of B. Then α(A) divides bij, so |bij| ≥α(A).
