1.12
∗The Computability of Homology Groups
We have computed the homology groups of some familiar spaces, such as the
sphere and the torus and the Klein bottle. Now we ask the question whether
one can in fact compute homology groups in general. For finite complexes,
the answer is affirmative. In this section, we present an explicit algorithm for
carrying out the computation.
First, we prove a basic theorem giving a “normal form” for homomorphisms
of finitely generated free abelian groups. The proof is constructive in nature.
One corollary is the theorem about subgroups of free abelian groups that we
stated earlier as Theorem 1.5.2. A second corollary is a theorem concerning
standard bases for free chain complexes. A third corollary gives our desired
algorithm for computing the homology groups of a finite complex.
First, we need two lemmas with which you might already be familiar.
Lemma 1.12.1 Let A be a free abelian group of rank n. If B is a subgroup
of A, then B is free abelian of rank r ≤n.
Proof: We may without loss of generality assume that B is a subgroup of the
n-fold direct product Zn = Z × Z × · · · × Z. We construct a basis for B as
follows:
Let πi : Zn →Z denote projection on the ith coordinate. For each m ≤n,
let Bm be the subgroup of B defined by the equation
Bm = B ∩(Zm × 0) .
That is, Bm consists of all x ∈B such that πi(x) = 0 for i > m. In particular,
Bn = B. Now the homomorphism
πm : Bm →Z
carries Bm onto a subgroup of Z. If this subgroup is trivial let xm = 0;
otherwise, choose xm ∈Bm so that its image πm(xm) generates this subgroup.
We assert that the non-zero elements of the set {x1, x2, . . . , xn} form a basis
for B.
First, we show that, for each m, the elements x1, x2, . . . , xm generate Bm.
[Then, in particular, the elements x1, x2, . . . , xn generate B.] We use induction
on m. It is trivial that x1 generates B1; indeed, if d is the integer π1(x1), then
x1 = (d, 0, 0, . . . , 0)
and B1 consists of all multiples of this element. [Even if x1 = 0 and d = 0,
B1 still consists of all multiples of x1.]
