8.7
A Second Proof of Poincar´e Duality
Now we give a second proof of the Poincar´e duality theorem. This time we
construct the duality isomorphism using cap products. There are several in-
teresting consequences of this procedure, including a naturality formula for
the duality isomorphism and a proof of its independence of the triangulation
involved.
Definition 8.7.1 Let X be a compact, triangulable homology n-manifold.
[Note that no specific triangulation of X is assumed.] Let Xi be a connected
component of X. If X is orientable, then Hn(Xi) is infinite cyclic. A generator
Γ(i) of Hn(Xi) is called an orientation class for Xi. The image of the classes
Γ(i) under the isomorphism induced by the inclusions
⊗Hn(Xi) ∼= Hn(X)
is called an orientation class for X and is denoted by Γ. Similarly, if X is
not necessarily orientable, and if Γ(i)
(2) is the unique non-trivial element of
Hn(Xi; Z/2), then the image of these elements in Hn(X; Z/2) is denoted by
Γ(2) and is called an orientation class for X over Z/2.
If X is given a specific triangulation, then Γ(i) is represented by the sum of
all the n-simplices of the connected component Xi suitably oriented. Similarly,
Γ(2) is represented by the sum of the n-simplices of X, each with coefficient
[1] ∈Z/2.
