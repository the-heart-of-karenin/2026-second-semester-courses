We define the notion of a quotient space as follows: Let X∗be a partition
of X into disjoint subsets whose union is X. Let π : X →X∗map each point
to the subset that contains it. Let us topologize X∗by declaring a set U ⊂X∗
to be open if π−1(U) ⊂X is open. Then π is a quotient map and we say that
X∗is a quotient space of X. We often say that this quotient is obtained by
identifying each element of the partition to a point.
If p : X →Y is a quotient map, then we can consider Y to be a quotient
space of X in the following manner: Given p, let X∗be the partition of X
into the disjoint sets p−1(y) for y ∈Y . Then X∗is homeomorphic to Y . We
now apply the preceding remark twice to obtain continuous maps g : X∗→Y
and h : Y →X∗as shown in the next diagram:

from I2 by identifying (s, 0) with (s, 1) and (0, t) with (1, t) for s, t ∈I.
