Corollary 2.8.8 Suppose w ∗K is a cone over the complex K. The map
π : |K| × I →|w ∗K| defined by
π(x, t) = (1 −t)x + tw
is a quotient map. It collapses |K| × I to the point w and is otherwise one-to-
one.
Proof: If σ = vo · · · vn is a simplex of K, let w∗σ denote the simplex wv0 · · · vn
of w ∗K. A set B is closed in |w ∗K| if its intersection with each simplex
w ∗σ is closed in that simplex. A set A is closed in |K| × I if its intersection
with each set σ × I is closed in σ × I. Therefore, for π to be a quotient map,
it suffices to show that the map
π′ : σ × I →w ∗σ ,
obtained by restricting π, is a quotient map. But that fact is immediate since
π′ is continuous and surjective, and the spaces involved are compact Hausdorff.
The preceding corollary suggests a way to define a cone over an arbitrary
topological space.
Definition 2.8.9 Let X be a space. We define the cone over X to be the
quotient space obtained from X × I by identifying the subset X × 1 to a
point. This point is called the vertex of the cone. The cone itself is denoted
by C(X). Formally, we define C(X) by partitioning X × I into the one-point
sets {(x, t)} for t < 1 and the set X × 1, and passing to the resulting quotient
space.
The next definition is used in Exercise 2.
Definition 2.8.10 The space obtained from two copies of R2, say R2 × {a}
and R2 × {b}, by identifying (x, a) and (x, b) whenever x̸ = 0 is called the
plane with two origins.
