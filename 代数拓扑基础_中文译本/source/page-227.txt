Elements of Algebraic Topology
Throughout this section, let C denote an arbitrary category with objects
X, Y, . . . and morphisms f, g, . . . , and let A denote the category of augmented
chain complexes and chain maps of such. For most of the applications we
have in mind, C will be either the topological category [whose objects are
topological spaces and whose morphisms are continuous maps] or the category
of pairs of spaces and pairs of continuous maps. So you may think only of those
categories if you wish.
We will be dealing with functors [see Definition 3.7.3] G and G′ from C to
A. Recall that if X is an element of C, then the functors assign to X objects
G(X) and G′(X) in A. Thus both G(X) and G′(X) are augmented chain
complexes. For each p ≥0, Gp(X) and G′
p(X) are chain groups. If Y is another
element of C, then we have two more chain complexes G(Y ) and G′(Y ), and
for each p ≥0, the corresponding chain groups Gp(Y ) and G′
p(Y ). The two
elements X and Y of C also bring into play the set of morphisms hom(X, Y )
in C. The functors G and G′ act on morphisms of C, so for f ∈hom(X, Y ),
the functors assign to f morphisms G(f) and G′(f) in A. In this case, the
morphisms G(f) : G(X) →G(Y ) and G′(f) : G′(X) →G′(Y ) are chain maps
of augmented chain complexes [see Definition 1.14.2].
