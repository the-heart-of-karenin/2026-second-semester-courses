8.6
Cap Products
To present our second proof of Poincar´e duality, we must first treat a topic
that is purely algebraic in nature. What we shall describe here is a certain
operation called the cap product of a cohomology class and a homology class.
Its definition is formally similar to the definition of cup product that we dis-
cussed in Section 5.9. Its usefulness, which lies in its connection with the
duality theorems, will become apparent shortly.
Throughout this section, let R be a commutative ring with unit element.
Definition 8.6.1 Let X be a topological space. We define a map
Sp(X; R) ⊗Sp+q(X; R) ⌢
−→Sq(X; R)
as follows: If T : ∆p+q →X is a singular simplex on X, and if α ∈R, then
cp ⌢(T ⊗α) = T ◦I(ϵ0, . . . , ϵq) ⊗α · ⟨cp, T ◦I(ϵq, . . . , ϵp+q)⟩.
This chain is called the cap product of the cochain cp and the chain T ⊗α.
