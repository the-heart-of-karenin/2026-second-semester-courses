Definition 4.14.4 Let us introduce an equivalence relation in the complex
n-sphere S2n+1 ⊂Cn+1 by defining
(z1, z2, . . . , zn+1, 0, . . . ) ∼(λz1, λz2, . . . , λzn+1, 0, . . . )
for each complex number λ with |λ| = 1. The resulting quotient space is called
complex projective n-space and is denoted CP n.
We show that the quotient map p : S2n+1 →CP n is a closed map. If A is
a closed set in S2n+1, then p−1p(A) is the image of S1 × A under the scalar
multiplication map (λ, z) →λ z. Since this map is continuous and S1 × A is
compact, its image is compact and therefore closed in S2n+1. Then p(A) is
closed in CP n, by definition.
It follows that CP n is Hausdorff [in fact normal].
As in the real case, Cn ⊂Cn+1 for all n, so that S2n−1 ⊂S2n+1. Then,
passing to quotient spaces, we have CP n−1 ⊂CP n. In fact, CP n−1 is a closed
subspace of CP n, by the same argument as before.
Said differently, the elements of CP n are equivalence classes of sequences
(z1, z2, . . . ) of complex numbers such that zi = 0 for i > n+1. If one sequence
in an equivalence class satisfies the equation zn+1 = 0, so does every mem-
ber of the equivalence class, and the class in question belongs to CP n−1, by
definition.
