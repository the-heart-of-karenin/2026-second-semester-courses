As an application, we compute the singular homology of the ball and the
sphere.
Theorem 4.4.11 Let n ≥0. The group Hi(Bn, Sn−1) is infinite cyclic for
i = n and vanishes otherwise. The group Hi(Sn) is infinite cyclic for i = n
and vanishes otherwise. The homomorphism of Hn(Sn) with itself induced by
the reflection map
ρ1(x1, x2, . . . , xn+1) = (−x1, x2, . . . , xn+1)
equals multiplication by −1.
Remark 4.4.12 Recalling Definition 2.9.1, we see that, for n ≥1, the theo-
rem tells us that the reflection map has degree −1.
Proof of the theorem: First we consider the case n = 0. Recall that B0
is the one-point space. Also, we must have S−1 = ∅, since points of the unit
sphere have norm 1. Theorem 4.3.3 then tells us that Hp(B0, ∅) is infinite
cyclic for p = 0 and vanishes otherwise.
It is easy to generalize the proof of Theorem 4.3.3 to a space consisting
of two points, such as S0 = {−1, +1}. One shows that Hp(S0) = 0 for p > 0
and that H0(S0) = Z⊕Z with generators T−1 and T+1. Consequently, H0(S0)
is infinite cyclic with generator T+1 −T−1. The map ρ1 on S0 exchanges
the two points −1 and +1, so the induced homomorphism of H0(S0) equals
multiplication by −1.
