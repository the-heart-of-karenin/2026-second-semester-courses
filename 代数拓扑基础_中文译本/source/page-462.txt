Notation 8.2.4 Let X and Y be topological spaces. We shall write X ≈Y
to indicate that X and Y are homeomorphic.
Corollary 8.2.5 Suppose that K ∗L and M ∗N are defined, where K is
locally finite. If |K| ≈|M| and |L| ≈|N|, then |K ∗L| ≈|M ∗N|.
Proof: Since |K| is locally compact, so is |M|. Thus M is locally finite. If
h : |K| →|M| and k : |L| →|N| are homeomorphisms, then h×k×iI induces,
via the quotient maps indicated in the preceding lemma, a homeomorphism
of |K ∗L| with |M ∗N| that equals h on |K| and k on |L|.
Lemma 8.2.6 Let J, K, and L be complexes. Assume that J ∗K and (J ∗
K) ∗L exist. Then J ∗K = K ∗J and (J ∗K) ∗L = J ∗(K ∗L).
Proof: The symmetry of the definition shows that J ∗K = K ∗J. Similarly,
if (J ∗K) ∗L is defined then for v0v1 . . . vm ∈J and w0w1 . . . wn ∈K and
x0x1 . . . xp ∈L the points
v0, v1, . . . , vm, w0, w1, . . . , wn, x0, x1, . . . , xp
are independent and the collection of simplices they span, along with faces of
such simplices, forms a complex. But this is just the complex J ∗(K ∗L).

Proof: In the case n = 1, |L| consists of two points and the complex K ∗L

consequence of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence [see Theorem 3.4.6].
In general, we suppose the theorem true in dimension n and prove induc-
