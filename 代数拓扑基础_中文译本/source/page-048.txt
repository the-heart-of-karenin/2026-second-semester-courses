EXAMPLE 1.6.7 For a 1-simplex, we compute ∂1[v0, v1] = v1 −v0. For a
2-simplex, one has
∂2[v0, v1, v2] = [v1, v2] −[v0, v2] + [v0, v1] .
For a 3-simplex, one has the formula
∂3[v0, v1, v2, v3] = [v1, v2, v3] −[v0, v2, v3] + [v0, v1, v3] −[v0, v1, v2] .
The geometric content of these formulas is pictured in Figure 1.6.2. If you re-
member the versions of Green’s, Stokes’, and Gauss’ theorem that you studied
in calculus, these pictures should look rather familiar.
EXAMPLE 1.6.8 Consider the 1 chain ∂2[v0, v1, v2] pictured in Fig-
ure 1.6.2. If you apply the operator ∂1 to this 1-chain, you get zero; everything
cancels out because each vertex appears as the initial point of one edge and
as the end point of another edge. You can check that a similar cancellation
occurs when you compute ∂2∂3[v0, v1, v2, v3].
