Theorem 8.3.3 Let (X, A) be a triangulated relative homology n-manifold.
Let s be a k-simplex of X that is not in A. If Lk s is empty, then k = n. If
Lk s is non-empty, then it has the homology of an n −k −1 sphere, where
n −k −1 ≥0. In either case, k ≤n.
Proof: Since s is not in A, its barycenter s lies in X \ A. Therefore the local
homology of X at s is infinite cyclic in dimension n, and vanishes otherwise.
If Lk s is empty, then by the preceding lemma, Hn(s, Bd s) ∼= Hn(X, X\s ),
which is nontrivial. Hence dim s = n. If Lk s is nonempty, then its reduced
homology is infinite cyclic in dimension n−k −1 and vanishes otherwise. This
implies in particular that n −k −1 ≥0 or that k < n.
Corollary 8.3.4 Let (X, A) be a triangulated relative homology n-manifold.
(a) The closure of X \ A equals a union of n-simplices.
(b) Every n−1 simplex s of X which is not in A is a face of precisely
two n-simplices of X.
Proof: (a) Let s be a k-simplex of X which is not in A. The preceding theorem
shows that k ≤n. If k < n then Lk s is a homology n −k −1 sphere, so it
contains an n −k −1 simplex t. Then s is in fact a face of the n-simplex s ∗t.
(b) If s is an (n −1)-simplex of X that is not in A, then Lk s is a homology
0-sphere. Since St s has dimension n and St s = s ∗Lk s, the complex Lk s
must have dimension 0. This means that Lk s consists of precisely two points.
Hence (b) holds.
