8.8
∗Application: Cohomology Rings of Manifolds
In this section, we assume familiarity with the projective spaces [Section 4.14].
We also use the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology [Section 7.3].
We now apply the Pioncar´e duality theorem to the problem of computing
the cohomology ring of a manifold. While we cannot compute the cohomology
ring of every compact manifold, the theorem we shall prove is strong enough to
enable us to compute the cohomology rings of the real and complex projective
spaces. This computation leads, in turn, to proofs of such classical theorems
of topology as the Borsuk-Ulam theorem and the so-called “Ham-Sandwich
Theorem.”
Definition 8.8.1 Let A and B be free abelian groups of the same rank. Let
C be an infinite cyclic group. We say that a homomorphism
f : A ⊗B →C
is a dual pairing if there are bases a1, . . . , am for A and b1, . . . , bm for B so
that
f(ai ⊗bj) = δijγ
for all i, j = 1, . . . , m, where γ is a generator of C.
