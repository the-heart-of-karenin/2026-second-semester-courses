Lemma 4.4.4 Let T : ∆p →σ be a linear homeomorphism of ∆p with the
p-simplex σ. Then each term of sd T is a linear homeomorphism of ∆p with
a simplex in the first barycentric subdivision of σ.
Proof: The lemma is trivial for p = 0. Now suppose the lemma is true in
dimensions less than p. First we consider the identity linear homeomorphism
ip : ∆p →∆p. We have
sd ip = [sd ∂ip, ∆p] .
Each term in ∂ip is a linear homeomorphism of ∆p−1 with a simplex in Bd ∆p.
By the induction hypotheses, sd (∂ip) =  ± Ti, where Ti is a linear homeo-
morphism of ∆p−1 with a simplex s1 . . . sp in the first barycentric subdivision
of Bd ∆p. Then [Ti, ∆p] is by definition a linear homeomorphism of ∆p with
the simplex ∆ps1 . . . sp, which belongs to the first barycentric subdivision of
∆p.
Now consider a general linear homeomorphism T : ∆p →σ. Note that T
defines a linear isomorphism of the first barycentric subdivision of ∆p with
the first barycentric subdivision of σ, because it carries the barycenter of ∆p
to the barycenter of σ. By definition, we have sd T = T#(sd ip). Since the
composition of linear homeomorphisms is a linear homeomorphism, each term
in sd T is a linear homeomorphism of ∆p with a simplex in the first barycentric
subdivision of σ.
