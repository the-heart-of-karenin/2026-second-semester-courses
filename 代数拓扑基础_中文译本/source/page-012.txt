Preface
Algebraic topology is a cornerstone of modern mathematics. Every working
mathematician should have at least an acquaintance with the subject. The
book Elements of Algebraic Topology by James R. Munkres is a classic that
provides such an introduction. It is now forty years old and needs to be brought
up to date. This new edition is meant to fulfill that purpose.
Of course, we have retained the essential features of the original book.
Most of the notation is the same and most of the terminology is the same. We
have retained all of the key topics from the first edition. But there are some
useful additions and changes.
Unlike the first edition, we do give an introduction to homotopy theory
in a section on the fundamental group. The section on acyclic models has
been completely revamped to make it more accessible to the reader. We have
collected together at the end some very limited biographical information about
each person mentioned in the text. There is also a short list of additional
readings, mostly chosen from books published after the first edition. Another
new feature is a very detailed Index of Notation. And the main Index for
the book is now also very detailed. Finally, we have added quite a number of
exercises. Most of these are in the nature of drill.
Organization of this book
The first two chapters are based on simplicial homology—the most concrete of
homology theories—so they should be quite accessible to students. The third
chapter introduces material that is more abstract, including the Eilenberg–
Steenrod Axioms. The fourth chapter introduces the singular homology theory
and many interesting applications. CW complexes appear as a computational
tool. For a one-semester course, these first four chapters would be suitable.
Sections marked with asterisks may be considered optional for this one-
semester course. Of course, the applications seem to the authors to be of great
interest, so while optional, the choice may well be to cover some or all of them.
