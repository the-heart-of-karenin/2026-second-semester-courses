5.7
∗Chain Equivalences in Free Chain Complexes
Results of this section will be used in Section 7.6 and in Section 7.10 in proving
the naturality of certain exact sequences.
We now prove a second version of Theorem 5.6.6. Again we assume we are
given a chain map ϕ : C →D of free chain complexes that induces homology
isomorphisms in all dimensions. We show that if C and D satisfy the fairly
mild additional condition that both vanish below a certain dimension, then
it follows not only that ϕ∗is an isomorphism, but that the chain map ϕ is
itself a chain equivalence. The proof involves the algebraic mapping cylinder
constructed in the last section.
First we need an elementary lemma, that for later use is stated in slightly
greater generality than we presently need.
Lemma 5.7.1 Let E and F be non-negative chain complexes. Suppose Ep is
free for p > 0 and Hp(F) = 0 for p > 0. Then any two chain maps f, g : E →F
that agree in dimension 0 are chain homotopic.
Proof: Define D : E0 →F1 to be zero. Then the equation ∂D + D∂= g −f
holds in dimension 0, because g = f in dimension 0. Suppose D is defined in
dimension p−1, where p > 0. Choose a basis for Ep. If e is a basis element, then
g(e)−f(e)−D(∂e) is well-defined, and it is a cycle [by the usual computation].
Define D(e) to be an element of Fp+1 whose boundary equals this cycle.
As you might suspect, one can derive this last result from the acyclic carrier
theorem. But care must be exercised, so it is not worth the effort.
Theorem 5.7.2 Let C and D be free chain complexes that vanish below a
certain dimension. Let ϕ : C →D be a chain map. If ϕ induces homology
isomorphisms in all dimensions, then ϕ is a chain equivalence.
Proof: We return to the chain complex D′ defined in the proof of Lemma 5.6.5.
The inclusion mapping
i : Cp →D′
p = Cp ⊕Dp ⊕Cp−1
is chain homotopic to j ◦ϕ, where j : Dp →D′
p is inclusion. Since j and ϕ
induce homology isomorphisms, so does i. It follows that the chain complex
E = D′/C has vanishing homology in all dimensions. Now we have
Ep ∼= Dp ⊕Cp−1 .
The induced boundary operator satisfies the formula
∂(dp, cp−1) = (∂dp + ϕ(cp−1), −∂cp−1) .
