What does the chain complex D′ look like algebraically? Suppose we break
Y ′ up into the cells of a CW complex. The open p-cells will consist of open
p-simplices Int σp of X, the open p-simplices of Int τp of Y , and the open cells
of the form π(Int σp−1 × Int I) that lie “between” X and Y . Then the pth
chain group of Y ′ is essentially just
Cp(X) ⊕Cp(Y ) ⊕Cp−1(X) ,
since the group of “in between” cells is isomorphic to Cp−1(X). How does the
boundary operator of Y ′ act on these chains? Clearly, it acts just like ∂X in
X, and ∂Y in Y . What does it do to a cell of the third kind? In the space
X × I, it is easy to see that
∂(σ × I) = σ × 0 −σ × 1 ± (∂σ) × I .
[One has to be careful with signs.] When X × 0 is pasted onto Y via h, this
formula becomes
∂′(Int σ × Int I) = h#(σ) −σ ± (∂σ) × I .
Finally, when we identify the p-chains of Y ′ with the group Cp(X) ⊕Cp(Y ) ⊕
Cp−1(X), this formula becomes
∂′(0, 0, σp−1) = (−σp−1, h#(σp−1), ±∂σp−1) .

Let us give one application of this theorem. It is a formula that relates
cohomology with homology. It will be generalized in Chapter 7.
Definition 5.6.9 If C = {Ci, ∂} is a chain complex, then there is a map
Hom (Cp, G) × Cp →G
that carries the pair (cp, cp) to the element ⟨cp, cp⟩of G. It is bilinear, and it
is called the evaluation map. It induces a bilinear map
Hp(C; G) × Hp(C) →G ,
that we call the Kronecker index. The image of αp and βp under the map is
also denoted by ⟨αp, βp⟩.
