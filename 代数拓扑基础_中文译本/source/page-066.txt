1.9
The Homology of a Cone
Now we compute the homology of the n-simplex and its boundary. A conve-
nient way of doing this is to introduce the notion of a cone.
Definition 1.9.1 Suppose that K is a complex in EJ, and w is a point of EJ
such that each ray emanating from w intersects |K| in at most one point. We
define the cone on K with vertex w to be the collection of all simplices of the
form wa0a1 . . . ap, where a0a1 . . . ap is a simplex of K, along with all faces of
such simplices. We denote this collection by w ∗K. See Figure 1.9.1.
We show that w ∗K is a well-defined complex, and it contains K as a
subcomplex; K is often called the base of the cone.
First we show that the set {wa0a1 . . . ap} is geometrically independent: If
w were in the plane P determined by a0, a1, . . . , ap, we could consider the line
segment joining w to an interior point x of σ = a0a1 . . . ap. The set Int σ,
being open in P, would contain an interval of points on this line segment. But
the ray from w through x intersects |K| in only one point, by hypothesis.
