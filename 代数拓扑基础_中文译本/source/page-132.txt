2.6
Topological Invariance of the
Homology Groups
In this section we achieve the main goal of this chapter: to prove the topological
invariance of the simplicial homology groups.
Definition 2.6.1 Let K and L be simplicial complexes. Let h : |K| →|L| be
a continuous map. Choose a subdivision K′ of K such that h has a simplicial
approximation f : K′ →L. Let λ : C(K) →C(K′) be the subdivision operator.
We define the homomorphism induced by h,
h∗: Hp(K) →Hp(L) ,
by the equation h∗= f∗◦λ∗.
Note that, once K′ has been chosen, the homomorphism h∗is independent
of the particular choice of the simplicial approximation f : K′ →L to h. For
any two such simplicial approximations are contiguous.
Observe also that if g : K′ →K is a simplicial approximation to the
identity map i|K| of |K| with itself, then λ∗and g∗are inverse to each other.
Therefore one could just as well define
h∗= f∗◦(g∗)−1 .
We use this fact to show that h∗is independent of the choice of the subdivision
K′. Suppose that K′′ is another subdivision of K such that h has a simplicial
approximation mapping of K′′ into L. We show that if h∗is defined using the
subdivision K′′, the result is the same as if one uses the subdivision K′.
