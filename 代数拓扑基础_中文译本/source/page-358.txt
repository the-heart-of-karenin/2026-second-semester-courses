5.8
The Cohomology of CW Complexes
Now we can compute the cohomology groups of some familiar spaces, and we
can find specific cocycles that generate these groups. The basic theorem we
shall use in carrying out these computations is the following:
Theorem 5.8.1 Let X be a CW complex. Let D(X) be its cellular chain
complex. Then
Hp(D(X); G) ∼= Hp(X; G)
for all p and G. If X is a triangulable CW complex, triangulated by a complex
K, then the isomorphism is induced by the inclusion D(X) →C(X).
Proof: Both D(X) and S(X) are free chain complexes. Since their homology
groups are isomorphic, so are their cohomology groups, by Corollary 5.6.7. In
the case where X is triangulable, the inclusion map i : D(X) →C(X) induces
the homology isomorphism in question. [See Theorem 4.13.9.] Then i induces
a cohomology isomorphism as well.
Corollary 5.8.2 Suppose n > 0. Then the following hold:
Hi(Sn; G)
∼=
G for i = 0 and i = n,
Hi(Bn, Sn−1; G)
∼=
G for i = n.
These cohomology groups vanish for other values of i.
Proof: The first statement follows from the fact that the cellular chain com-
plex of Sn is infinite cyclic in dimensions 0 and n and vanishes otherwise, and
all the boundary operators vanish. The second then follows from the long exact
sequence in reduced cohomology, using the fact that the reduced cohomology
of Bn vanishes, since it is contractible.
