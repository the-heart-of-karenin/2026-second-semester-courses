To illustrate how the Mayer–Vietoris sequence is used in practice, we shall
now compute the homology of the suspension of a complex. The suspension of
a complex appeared earlier in Definition 1.9.8, but for convenience, we repeat
it here.
Definition 3.4.5 Let K be a complex. Let w0 ∗K and w1 ∗K be two cones
on K whose polytopes intersect in |K| alone. Then
S(K) = (w0 ∗K) ∪(w1 ∗K)
is a complex. It is called a suspension of K. Given K, the complex S(K) is
uniquely defined up to a simplicial isomorphism.
Theorem 3.4.6 If K is a complex, then for all p there is an isomorphism
Hp(S(K)) →Hp−1(K) .
Proof: Let K0 = w0 ∗K and K1 = w1 ∗K. Then K0 ∪K1 = S(K) and
K0 ∩K1 = K. In the reduced Mayer–Vietoris sequence,
Hp(K0) ⊕Hp(K1) −→Hp(S(K)) −→Hp−1(K) −→Hp−1(K0) ⊕Hp−1(K1);
both end terms vanish just because K0 and K1 are cones. Therefore the middle
map is an isomorphism.
Theorem 3.4.7 Let K be a complex. Let K0 and K1 be subcomplexes of K.
Let L0 and L1 be subcomplexes of K0 and K1 respectively. There exists an
exact sequence
· · · −→Hi(K0 ∩K1, L0 ∩L1) −→Hi(K0, L0) ⊕Hi(K1, L1)
Hi(K0 ∪K1, L0 ∪L1) −→· · · .
Definition 3.4.8 The sequence in this last theorem is called the relative
Mayer–Vietoris sequence.
