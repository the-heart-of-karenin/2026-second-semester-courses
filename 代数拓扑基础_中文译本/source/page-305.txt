We have noted that CP 0 is a single point. The space CP 1 is obtained by
attaching a 2-cell to CP 0; therefore, CP 1 is homeomorphic to S2.
Definition 4.14.6 Consider the increasing sequence CP 0 ⊂CP 1 ⊂. . . of
projective spaces. Their coherent union is denoted by CP ∞, and is called
infinite-dimensional complex projective space . By Theorem 4.12.15, it is a
CW complex with one cell in each non-negative even dimension, and CP n is
its 2n-skeleton.
The homology of complex projective space is exceedingly easy to compute:
Theorem 4.14.7 The group Hi(CP n) is infinite cyclic if i is even and 0 ≤
i ≤2n, and it vanishes otherwise. The group Hi(CP ∞) is infinite cyclic if i
is even and i ≥0, and it vanishes otherwise.
Proof: The cellular chain group Di(CP n) is infinite cyclic if i is even and
0 ≤i ≤2n, and otherwise it vanishes. Therefore, every chain of this chain
complex is a cycle, and no chain bounds. A similar computation applies to
CP ∞.
The computation of the homology of real projective space is much more
difficult. The cellular chain group Dk(Pn) is infinite cyclic for 0 ≤k ≤n, so we
cannot avoid a detailed examination of the boundary operator in the cellular
complex. Since the open k-cell ek of Pn equals Pk \ Pk−1, and ˙ek = Pk−1, we
have Dk(Pn) = Hk(Pk, Pk−1). Thus we must compute the boundary operator
∂∗: Hk+1(Pk+1, Pk) →Hk(Pk, Pk−1) .
