Theorem 6.3.1 If C and D are free chain complexes, and if the chain map
ϕ : C →D induces homology isomorphisms in all dimensions, then so does the
chain map
ϕ ⊗iG : C ⊗G →D ⊗G .
Proof:
Step 1. We first consider the case where we have a short exact
sequence
0 −→C −→D −→E −→0
of free chain complexes. We know that Hp(E) = 0 for all p, and we wish to
prove that Hp(E; G) = 0 for all p. As in the proof of Lemma 5.6.4, we can
write Ep = Bp ⊕Up, where ∂maps Bp to zero and carries Up isomorphically
onto Bp−1. Then we have
Ep ⊗G ∼= (Bp ⊗G) ⊕(Up ⊗G) ,
and ∂⊗iG maps Bp ⊗G to zero and carries Up ⊗G isomorphically onto
Bp−1 ⊗G. It follows that Hp(E; G) = 0 for all p.
Step 2. The general case now follows from Lemma 5.6.5. Given ϕ : C →D,
there are a chain complex D′ and injective chain maps i : C →D′ and j : D →
D′ such that j induces homology isomorphisms in all dimensions and j ◦ϕ is
chain homotopic to i. Furthermore, D′, D′/im i, and D′/im j are free. If ϕ∗
is an isomorphism in ordinary homology, so is i∗= j∗◦ϕ∗. Then by Step
1, both i and j induce isomorphisms of homology with arbitrary coefficients.
Hence ϕ does the same.
Simplicial Homology
Let (K, K0) be a simplicial pair, and let G be an abelian group. We define the
simplicial homology of (K, K0) with coefficients in G by the equation
Hp(K, K0; G) = Hp(C(K, K0); G) .
We define the reduced groups by the equation
Hp(K; G) = Hp({C(K), ϵ}; G) ,
where ϵ is the usual augmentation for C(K).
Now the group Cp(K, K0) ⊗G is the direct sum of copies of G, one copy
for each p-simplex of K not in K0. Indeed, if we orient the p-simplices σα of K
not in K0 arbitrarily, then each element of Cp(K, K0) ⊗G can be represented
uniquely by a finite sum  σα ⊗gα. Its boundary is then represented by
(∂σα) ⊗gα.
