Homology with Coefficients
6.1
Introduction
After having studied cohomology with arbitrary coefficients, we now return to
a subject that was briefly introduced for the simplicial theory in Chapter 1—
homology with arbitrary coefficients.
To start with, we introduce an algebraic functor called the tensor prod-
uct and study its properties. This functor plays a role in homology theory
that is similar to that played by the Hom functor for cohomology. After that
introduction, we study homology with arbitrary coefficients.
6.2
Tensor Products
If A and B are abelian groups, then of course their Cartesian product A×B is
an abelian group, and one often considers homomorphisms of the group A×B
into a third abelian group C. Sometimes it is important to consider functions
from A × B to C that are bilinear, that is, functions that are homomorphisms
when considered as function of each variable separately. In this section, we
define an abelian group called the tensor product of A and B, denoted A ⊗B,
that has the property that bilinear functions from A × B to C can naturally
be considered as homomorphisms from A ⊗B to C, and conversely. Thus the
study of bilinear functions is reduced to the familiar study of homomorphisms.
Definition 6.2.1 Let A and B be abelian groups. Let F(A, B) be the free
abelian group generated by the set A × B. Let R(A, B) be the subgroup
generated by all elements of the form
(a + a′, b) −(a, b) −(a′, b) ,
(a, b + b′) −(a, b) −(a, b′) ,
for a, a′ ∈A and b, b′ ∈B. The subgroup R(A, B) is called the relations
subgroup. We define
A ⊗B = F(A, B)/R(A, B) ,
