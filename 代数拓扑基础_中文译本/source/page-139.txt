Theorem 2.7.5 If the maps h, k : |K| →|L| are homotopic, then the homo-
morphisms h∗, k∗: Hp(K) →Hp(L) are equal. The same holds for reduced
homology.
Proof: Let K be a complex. Let M be a complex whose underlying space is
|K| × I, such that for each σ ∈K, both σ × 0 and σ × 1 are simplices of M.
Also suppose that σ × I is the polytope of a subcomplex of M.
Let F : |K| × I →|L| be the homotopy of h to k. Let i, j : |K| →|K| × I
be the maps i(x) = (x, 0) and j(x) = (x, 1), as shown in Figure 2.7.4. Then i
and j are simplicial maps of K into the complex M. Furthermore,
F ◦i = h
and
F ◦j = k .

We claim that the chain maps i# and j# induced by i and j are chain
homotopies. Consider the function Φ assigning to each simplex σ of K the
subcomplex of M whose polytope is σ × I. Now the space σ × I is acyclic
because it is homeomorphic to a closed ball. And if σ ≻s, then s × I ⊂σ × I
so Φ(s) is a subcomplex of Φ(σ). Hence Φ is an acyclic carrier from K to
M. Furthermore, it carries both i# and j# because both i(σ) = σ × 0 and
j(σ) = σ × 1 belong to Φ(σ). It follows from Theorem 1.14.10 that i# and j#
are chain homotopic. We conclude that i∗= j∗. Thus
h∗= F∗◦i∗= F∗◦j∗= k∗
as desired.
This result carries over directly to the relative homology groups. Given
maps h, k : (|K|, |K0|) →(|L|, |L0|), we say that they are homotopic (as maps
of pairs of spaces) if there is a homotopy H : |K| × I →|L| of h to k such
that H maps |K0| × I into |L0|. Now we have the following theorem:
Theorem 2.7.6 If h and k are homotopic as maps of pairs of spaces, then
h∗= k∗as maps of relative homology groups.
