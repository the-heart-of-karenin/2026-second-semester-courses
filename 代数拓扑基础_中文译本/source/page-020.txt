Definition 1.2.3
(a) Let σ be the simplex spanned by the geometrically independent
points a0, a1, . . . , an. The points ai are called the vertices of σ and
the number n is called the dimension of σ. One can denote σ by
a0a1 . . . an.
(b) Any simplex spanned by a subset of {a0, a1, . . . , an} is called a
face of σ.
(c) Let s be the face of σ spanned by a0, a1, . . . , ap [where p < n];
and let t be the face spanned by ap+1, ap+2, . . . , an. Then t is called
the face of σ opposite s. In particular, the face of σ spanned by
a1, a2, . . . , an is called the face opposite a0.
(d) The faces of σ different from σ itself are called the proper faces
of σ.
(e) The union of the proper faces of σ is called the boundary of σ
and is denoted Bd σ.
(f) The interior of σ, denoted Int σ is defined by the equation
Int σ = σ \ Bd σ. It is important to note that the interior of a
0-simplex is the 0-simplex itself—a fact that may be contrary to
your intuition.
(g) The set Int σ is sometimes called an open simplex.

Definition 1.2.4 A subset A of RN is said to be convex if, for each pair x, y
of points in A, the line segment joining them lies in A.
There is also a weaker definition that is sometimes useful:
Definition 1.2.5 A subset A of RN is said to be star-convex relative to a
point w ∈A if, for each point x in A different from w, the line segment
joining x to w lies in A.
