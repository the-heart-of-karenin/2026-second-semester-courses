1.4
Abstract Simplicial Complexes
In practice, specifying a polyhedron X by giving a collection of simplices whose
union is X is not a very convenient way of dealing with specific polyhedra. One
quickly gets immersed in details of analytic geometry; it is messy to specify
all the simplices and to make sure they intersect only when they should. It
is much easier to specify X by means of an “abstract simplicial complex,” a
notion we now introduce.
Definition 1.4.1 An abstract simplicial complex is a collection S of finite
nonempty sets, such that if A is an element of S, then so is every nonempty
subset of A.
The element A of S is called a simplex of S; its dimension is one less than
the number of its elements. Each nonempty subset of A is called a face of A.
