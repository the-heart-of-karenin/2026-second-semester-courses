Definition 2.4.3 Let K be a complex and let K0 be a subcomplex. We define
a sequence of subdivisions of the skeleton of K as follows: Let J0 = K(0). In
general, suppose that Jp is a subdivision of the p-skeleton of K and each
simplex of K0 of dimension at most p belongs to Jp. Define Jp+1 to be the
union of the complex Jp, all (p + 1)-simplices σ belonging to K0, and the
cones σ ∗Jσ, as σ ranges over all (p + 1)-simplices of K not in K0. [Here Jσ
is the subcomplex of Jp whose polytope is Bd σ.] The union of the complexes
Jp is a subdivision of K, denoted sd(K/K0) and called the first barycentric
subdivision of K, holding K0 fixed.
As with barycentric subdivisions, this process can now be repeated. The
complex sd(sd(K/K0)/K0) will be called the second barycentric subdivision
of K, holding K0 fixed, and will be denoted by sd2(K/K0) and so forth.

To prove the general simplicial approximation theorem, iterated barycen-
tric subdivisions will not suffice, just because the Lebesgue number argument
used in the proof of Theorem 2.4.2 requires the space |K| to be compact. For a
general complex K, the number λ that measures how finely a simplex must be
subdivided may vary from one simplex to another. Thus we must generalize
our notion of barycentric subdivision to allow for this possibility.
