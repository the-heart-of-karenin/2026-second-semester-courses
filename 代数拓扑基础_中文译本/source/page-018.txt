It is elementary to check that, if {a0, a1, . . . , an} is geometrically in-
dependent, and if w lies outside the plane that these points span, then
{w, a0, a1, . . . , an} is geometrically independent.
An affine transformation T of RN is a map that is a composition of trans-
lations [i.e., maps of the form T(x) = x + p for fixed p], and non-singular
linear transformations. If T is an affine transformation, it is immediate from
the definitions that T preserves geometrically independent sets and that T
carries the plane P spanned by {a0, a1, . . . , an} onto the plane spanned by
{T(a0), T(a1), . . . , T(an)}.
Now the translation T(x) = x −a0 carries P onto the vector subspace of
RN having a1 −a0, a2 −a0, . . . , an −a0 as a basis. If we follow T by a linear
transformation of RN carrying a1 −a0, a2 −a0, . . . , an −a0 to the first n unit
basis vectors ϵ1, ϵ2, . . . , ϵn in RN, we obtain an affine transformation S of RN
such that S(s0) = 0 and S(ai) = ϵi for i > 0. The map S carries P onto the
plane Rn × 0 of the first n coordinates in RN. It is thus clear why we call P
a “plane of dimension n” in RN.
