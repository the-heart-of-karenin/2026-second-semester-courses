Nowadays we view the matter differently. We consider the cohomology
ring as the natural object and view the existence of the homology intersection
ring for manifolds as a happy accident resulting from the Poincar´e duality
isomorphism.
We are not going to study the homology intersection ring in any detail. We
shall, however, prove one lemma interpreting the ring operation as a geometric
intersection of cycles. This lemma will enable us to make computations in the
cohomology rings of lens spaces.
Notation 8.9.1 Throughout this section, X will denote a compact, con-
nected, triangulable n-manifold.
(a) In case X is orientable, we let Γ denote an orientation class for
X, and we let R denote an arbitrary commutative ring with unity.
(b) In case X is not orientable, we let Γ, denote the class Γ(2), and
we let R denote Z/2.
Definition 8.9.2 We define the intersection product of two homology classes
of X as follows. Given αp ∈Hp(X; R) and βq ∈Hq(X; R), choose αn−p and
βn−q so that
αn−p ⌢Γ = αp
and
βn−q ⌢Γ = βq .
Then define
αp · βq = (αn−p ∪βn−q) ⌢Γ .
Note that when X is orientable, the sign of the result depends on the
orientation class Γ. Notice also that the product of αp and βq has dimension
p+q−n. This fact makes the connection with geometric intersection plausible,
for this is just the dimension of the intersection in Rn of a general plane of
dimension p and a general plane of dimension q.

of the q-simplex σ of X. If dq has coefficient ±a on the simplex σ, and cp has
