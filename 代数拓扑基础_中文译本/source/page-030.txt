Homology Groups of a Simplicial Complex

Let J be an arbitrary index set, and let RJ denote the J-fold cartesian
product of R with itself. An element of RJ is a function from J to R, ordinarily
denoted using the “tuple notation” (xα)α∈J. The product RJ is of course a
vector space with the usual component-wise addition and multiplication by
scalars.
Let EJ denote the subset of RJ consisting of all points (xα)α∈J such that
xα = 0 for all but finitely many values of α. Note that EJ is a vector subspace
of (xα)α∈J. If ϵα ∈EJ is the map of J into R whose value is 1 on the index α
and is 0 on all other elements of J, then the set {ϵα : α ∈J} is a basis for EJ
[but not for RJ, if J is infinite].
We call EJ generalized euclidean space and topologize it by the metric
|x −y| = max
α∈J |xα −yα| .
Everything we have done for complexes in RN generalizes to complexes in
EJ. The space EJ is the union of certain of its finite-dimensional subspaces—
namely, the subspaces spanned by finite subsets of the basis {ϵα : α ∈J}.
Each such subspace is simply a copy of RN for some N. Any finite set
{a0, a1, . . . , an} of points of EJ lies in such a subspace. If the points are geo-
metrically independent, then the simplex they span lies in the same subspace.
Furthermore, the metric on EJ gives the usual topology on each such sub-
space. Therefore, any finite collection of simplices in EJ lies in a copy of RN
for some N. All that we are really doing is to allow ourselves to deal with
complexes for which the total collection of simplices cannot be fitted entirely
into any one RN. Conceptually it may seem more complicated to work in EJ
than in RN, but in practice, it causes no difficulty at all.
We leave it to you to check that our results hold for complexes in EJ. We
shall use these results freely henceforth.
Let us make one further comment. If K is a complex in RN, then each
simplex of K has dimension at most N. On the other hand, if K is a complex
in EJ, there need be no upper bound on the dimensions of the simplices of K.
We define the dimension of K, denoted dim K, to be the largest dimension
of a simplex of K, if such a maximum exists; otherwise, we say that K has
infinite dimension.
